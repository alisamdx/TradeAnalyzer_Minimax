# v1.1.98 — 2026-07-02

## Liquidity / open-interest in the Payoff Pre-Trade Validator

### Why
The user (discretionary wheel/CSP trader) asked to "bake in open interest so we focus on
liquidity as well" in the Payoff screen's validate function. A structure can look great on
paper — good cushion, right IV regime, sensible strikes — and still be a bad trade if the
options are illiquid: thin open interest and wide bid/ask spreads mean poor fills, heavy
slippage, and a hard time exiting. The validator had no liquidity dimension at all, and the
option legs didn't even carry open interest.

### What
1. **`PayoffLeg` now carries liquidity data** — `openInterest`, `volume`, `bid`, `ask`
   (optional/nullable). Populated in `addLegFromContract` when a leg is built from the live
   chain; manually-entered legs leave them undefined (liquidity genuinely unknown).
2. **New deterministic check #8 (liquidity)** in `pretrade-checks.ts`, judged by the **worst
   leg**:
   - Any option leg with open interest `< minOpenInterest` (100) → **warn** (thin OI).
   - Widest bid/ask spread `> maxSpreadPct` (20% of mid) → **warn** (poor fills).
   - Otherwise → **pass** ("Lowest leg open interest is N contracts … tradable").
   - No leg carries OI (manual entry) → **info** "Liquidity unknown" — not a false pass.
   - Rolls into the existing verdict (`warn` → caution), so a thin-OI trade downgrades the
     structure's overall verdict.
3. **Open interest shown per leg** in the legs list (next to Δ / IV).
4. **AI "Assess Trade" is liquidity-aware too** — per-leg OI/volume/bid-ask is added to the
   assessment context and the system prompt now lists liquidity/execution risk as a
   consideration, with an explicit guard to only cite liquidity figures actually provided (no
   fabrication — same discipline as the critic-context rule in CLAUDE.md).

### Thresholds (tunable, exported)
`PRETRADE_THRESHOLDS.minOpenInterest = 100`, `PRETRADE_THRESHOLDS.maxSpreadPct = 20`.
First-pass retail-liquidity floors; conservative enough to avoid over-flagging cheap options
while still catching genuinely untradeable strikes.

### Notes
- The spread threshold (20% of mid) intentionally trails OI as the primary signal, since cheap
  options naturally show wide % spreads; OI is the star, spread is the supporting flag.
- Decision-support only — no orders, not wired to the execution engine.
