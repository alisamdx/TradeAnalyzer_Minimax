## The first run inverted its own headline

`fr-spread-dte-cost-study.mjs` on 296 paired ticker-runs (captures to 2026-09-22):

| bucket | n | median DTE | monthly | mid credit | credit ÷ width | round-trip cost | **cost share** | fails 25% | OI short/long |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 21 DTE | 296 | 22 | **11%** | $2.17 | 26.9% | $0.75 | **36.7%** | 194 | **37 / 42** |
| 30 DTE | 296 | 31 | **69%** | $2.42 | 28.5% | $0.44 | **23.9%** | 140 | **460 / 565** |

Paired difference **+13.6%**, the shorter expiry cheaper on only 27% of pairs. Read alone that is a
decisive win for 30 DTE.

**The monthly control says otherwise.** The buckets differ 11% / 69% in monthly share, and on the 60
pairs where both buckets are the same expiry type the median difference is **−4.9%** — *the sign
flips*. Open interest tells the same story: 12× more depth at 30 DTE. And credit barely moves ($2.17
vs $2.42; 26.9% vs 28.5% of width), so the gap is not that a shorter expiry collects less — it is that
the quotes are wider.

So the headline is an **expiry-type** finding wearing a DTE costume: "nearest 30" lands on a monthly
two times in three, "nearest 21" lands on a weekly nine times in ten. This is the 2026-08-04
expiry-selection trap reproducing on a different instrument, exactly as the harness header predicted —
and the magnitudes corroborate it independently, since that study measured monthly-vs-weekly friction
at 19.6% vs 40.3% of credit against this one's 23.9% vs 36.7% on real spread quotes.

## What was added, and why each

- **The expiry-type 2×2 of pairs.** The "−4.9%" had to be interpreted by a calendar argument held in
  the reader's head: both buckets landing on a monthly is impossible whenever they are closer together
  than two third Fridays (28–35 days), and with ±4 tolerance these are at most 17 days apart — so the
  same-type comparison is weekly-vs-weekly and there is **no monthly-vs-monthly evidence at any n**.
  That deduction decides how the number reads, so it is printed, with the cell counts beside it,
  rather than left to be re-derived.
- **A sign count on every subgroup median.** A median with no sign count is a point estimate nobody
  has looked at the distribution of. Subgroups under 10 pairs now say "too few to say anything"
  instead of quoting a number — the QC-907 shape, where no signal and a weak signal must not look
  alike.
- **Conditional on both buckets clearing the 25% gate.** This is the half that killed the wheel's
  expiry lever on 2026-08-04: the friction gap was real (19.6% vs 40.3%) and **vanished conditional on
  clearing the entry gate** (6.9% vs 6.0%) — the whole gap sat in structures the engine already
  refused, so the saving was never payable. The same question applies here and the first run could not
  answer it: 78 pairs clear at both DTEs, and whether a difference survives among *those* is what
  decides BPS-4.

## Also visible in the first run, for BPS-9
