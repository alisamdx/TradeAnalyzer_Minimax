# v1.1.157 — 2026-09-02

## feat: capital-efficiency metrics — return on capital actually at risk

### Why

Measuring a wheel book against total portfolio value understates it whenever capital is held
back on purpose. Measured over 2026-04-09 → 08-28:

```
realized                       $2,957.66
  return on portfolio (~$79k)      3.73%
  return on avg deployed (~$26k)  11.56%     ← what the trading actually earned
SPY, same window                  13.15%
```

Those are different claims. The first describes a *sizing* decision (33% utilization, flat on 33
of 99 trading days); only the second describes the trading. Reporting one without the other turns
a deliberate choice into an apparent strategy failure — which is exactly the wrong conclusion
this book had been drawing.

### Design decisions

**Capital at risk per position.** CSP secures `strike × 100 × qty`; stock is cost basis; a long
option is its debit; **a covered call contributes zero** — the shares securing it are already
counted, and charging both would double-count the same dollars. That is what makes a wheel cycle
read as one continuous block of capital rather than several.

**Assignment hands capital over, it does not duplicate it.** An assigned CSP's capital ends where
the resulting stock lot begins. Detected by a stock position in the same ticker whose cost basis
matches `strike − premium` (TSLA: 350 − 5.00 = 345.00 against an actual 345.0052) — the
arithmetic signature of assignment, and hard to hit by accident. Without it the two overlap and
double-count ~$35k for a day.

**Idle days are included in the average, deliberately.** The metric answers *"for every dollar-day
at risk, what did I earn?"* Holding cash lowers the average and so raises return-per-deployed
dollar, while `return on portfolio` beside it shows what sitting flat cost. Both are always shown
together.

### The per-trade metric is where the learning is

For every closed position, both are computed from stored data:

```
projected at entry:  (premium / capital) × 365 / DTE_at_entry
realized:            (realized_pnl / capital) × 365 / days_actually_held
```

That separates a disciplined early close from a panicked one **without any counterfactual price
path**. From the real book: AMD $450 put, **+$858.97 on $45,000 in 7 days = ~+100%/yr**, which
*beat* the ~65%/yr its premium implied. NVDA $195 put, **−$169.03 on $19,500 in 2 days =
−158%/yr**. Similar dollar magnitudes, opposite decision quality — invisible in raw P&L.

On this book **10 of 19 short-premium closes beat their own projection**, so the early exits are
genuinely mixed rather than uniformly premature.

Annualization is clamped at ±999% for display on very short holds, flagged with `*`; dollar P&L
is never clamped. Long options get no projection — they pay a debit rather than collecting
premium.

### Surface

New **📈 Performance** tab on the Portfolio view: four headline stats plus a sortable per-trade
table of projected vs realized annualized ROC. Pure derivation from `positions` — no sync, no
provider call, no new API surface. Account value is inferred from the median of
`market_value ÷ pct_of_portfolio` across synced rows, so one stale row cannot skew it.

### Tests

New `tests/capital-efficiency.test.ts` (15): CC charges zero capital; assignment handoff ends the
put where the lot begins and does not double-count across the handoff day; idle days included in
the average with both returns reported; the real book's 3.61% vs 11.02% reproduced; a fast
efficient close scores above its own projection; a quick loss scores sharply negative; same-day
round trips count as one day rather than dividing by zero; absurd annualizations clamp but flag;
long options get no projection; open positions are skipped. Suite: 1035 passed.
