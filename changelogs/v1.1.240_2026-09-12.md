## What

A **Rollability** panel on the Roll / Closure Analyzer, implementing the "rollability check" from Bec's strategy (`docs/Bec/STRATEGY-SUMMARY.md` §6b, ranked most mechanisable in §9.1):

> Before accepting a strike, look at the cost to buy back the first in-the-money call, then look forward one week, two weeks, a month: how many strikes up can that same credit buy you? That tells you how far into the future you would have to go to get back above your break-even. If the answer is a year, the trade is a trap.

## Why the existing analyzer could not answer it

`RollAnalyzerService` fetched the current chain plus the next 6 expirations and ranked net-credit rolls inside 0.80–1.15 × strike. Six weeklies is about six weeks — a trap shows itself at 3, 6 or 12 months — and ranking candidates by a yield metric never produces "how far out until the strike is safe".

## Definition (`docs/formulas.md#rollability`)

- Target: covered call → stock cost basis if given, else underlying; short put → underlying.
- Horizons: listed expiry nearest +7/+14/+30/+60/+90/+180/+365 added days, deduplicated.
- Best strike per horizon: most-improved same-type strike, two-sided, `mid − buyToCloseMid > 0`. Worse strikes never count.
- Weeks to target: first horizon reaching the target, `addedDte / 7`; 0 when already there.
- Verdict: easy ≤ 4 wk; workable ≤ 13 wk; trap beyond, or unreached with ≥ 180 added days priced; unknown otherwise.

## Scope guards

- Reporting only; no ranking, gating, or engine input.
- Candidate table unchanged: `buildRollCandidates` still receives only the first 6 expiries; the far chains go only to `computeRollability`.
- Cost basis input only for calls; prefilled from the open `Stock` row for the ticker (`costPerShare ?? entryPrice`).
