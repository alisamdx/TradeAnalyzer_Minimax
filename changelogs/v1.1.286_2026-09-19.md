## R6 re-fetches the oldest findings first, and the gate email says what it did

### What happened to WULF

WULF's two QC-204 findings (2022-03-11 +49 %, 2025-08-14 +60 %) sat open with an empty `remediations_json` after R6's first live run, on 2026-09-18 (run 22). R6 had not failed on WULF; it had never tried:

- R6 takes at most `R6_MAX_TICKERS_PER_RUN` = 60 tickers per run and attempts each finding once, so the queue drains over successive runs.
- Candidates were ordered by **key**, which is alphabetical. Run 22 attempted 60 tickers (121 findings) and deferred the rest, which among open tickers was only WULF; JAN and SOLS had been settled by the reuse rulings.
- R6's report counted `deferred: 1`, but it went into the job result only. Neither the gate email nor the day report rendered it.

A rehearsal with a simulated successful R6 on WULF closes all four of its findings (QC-204 and QC-501, both dates) as `false_positive` verified genuine moves. Both bars come from the 2026-09-11 adjusted fetch and both steps are over 90 days old. The 2025-08-14 jump matches TeraWulf's Fluidstack/Google deal that day. Monday's run reaches WULF with no change needed.

### Changes

**Oldest first.** `r6Candidates` orders tickers by their oldest unattempted finding: lowest `first_seen_run_id`, then lowest `exception_id`, then ticker. The cap now drains the queue in the order findings arrived, and the order stays deterministic.

**In the email.** `buildStatusEmail` takes an optional `r6` summary, and `runDaily` passes it. The email gains a line after DOMAINS:

- `REMEDIATION  R6 (splits/dividends re-fetch): 60 tickers (59 ok, 1 failed), 121 findings marked · 1 deferred to a later run (cap 60 per run, oldest first)`
- `… : nothing to re-fetch` when there was nothing to do
- `… did not run: <error>` when R6 threw, which is already in the run's phase errors and is now also where the operator reads

The summary type is declared in `qa/email.ts` rather than importing the collector's `R6Report`, so the QA module does not depend on collector types. A caller that runs no remediation, such as the rehearsal script, passes nothing and gets no line.
