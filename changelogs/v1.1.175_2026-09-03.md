# v1.1.175 — 2026-09-03

## Stage-4 candidates are persisted — the brief stops discarding the losers

The brief keeps only the top `PICKS_PER_STRATEGY` (5) of a `STAGE4_POOL_SIZE` (15) pool, so the
cut from 15 to 5 has never been measurable. **When a ranking's quality is in question, the losers
are the data you need**, and this pipeline threw them away.

That matters now. v1.1.173 found the wheel composite **anti-predictive at the top** (mean return
on capital +1.91% in the 58–63 band against −4.99% at 73+), and v1.1.174 showed the dice tiebreak
is nearly vacuous — it fires on 7 of 62 days. So the top-5 cut is the remaining exposure, and it
could not be tested retroactively, only from the moment capture begins.

### Why a slim new table rather than keeping briefs longer

Measured on the live database:

| | |
|---|---|
| `daily_briefs` | **52.8 MB** across 366 rows — **9% of the 567 MB database** |
| per row | **147.7 KB** (each carries the full `strategyLabResult` per pick) |
| unpruned growth | **~229 MB per trading year** |

That is why `pruneStaleBriefs(90)` hard-deletes them on every run — and, in turn, **why every
study in this project reports "one regime"**: the evidence is destroyed at 90 days.

`brief_candidates` stores what a settlement study needs — ~90 rows/day, about **20 MB per trading
year**, roughly a tenth of what the briefs alone would cost. The briefs keep their 90-day window
for the UI while the evidence outlives it. Retention is **730 days**, purely so the table cannot
grow without bound.

### Why it captures more than the cut strictly needs

**Capture is one-directional: what is not written today cannot be recovered.** Three studies in
this session were blocked by capture rather than analysis — the liquidity A/B had to run on income
because wheel rows carried no bid/ask/open_interest until v1.1.132 (and wheel *still* cannot be
tested until October), the ETF-deflation gate was unsatisfiable because no ETF outcome had ever
been generated, and this cut discarded its losers. Every study also ended on the same follow-up:
**which signal caused it?** — which on T2 could only be inferred from weights and the worst tickers.

So the schema takes everything already computed at Stage 4, while it is free:

| captured | why |
|---|---|
| `signals_json` (~689 B) | turns "the composite selected badly" into "**this signal** selected badly" |
| `delta` | v1.1.171 showed delta drives outcomes (+0.65pp per matched-pair episode) — a selection study must control for it |
| `bid` / `ask` / `open_interest` | exactly what v1.1.132 had to bolt on, and whose absence still blocks wheel liquidity testing |
| `pop_estimate`, `annualized_return`, `max_loss`, `net_credit`, `atm_iv`, `grade` | the trade economics, at no extra call |

### What is captured

One row per Stage-4 candidate per strategy per date, with `disposition` recording how far it got:

| disposition | meaning |
|---|---|
| `picked` | survived into the brief's top 5 |
| `capped_out` | cleared the liquidity gate, outranked by the cap |
| `failed_liquidity` | rejected by `hasAdequateLiquidity` |
| `not_validated` | reached the queue, Stage-4 validation produced no lab result |

Plus composite, rank, and the structure the strategy would actually trade — slug, strike, premium,
expiration, DTE, liquidity — chosen through the same `pickRelevantStructure` the report, critic and
renderer use, so a stored candidate is directly comparable to a stored pick. Disposition is derived
from the same three sets the existing funnel log already computes, so the table and the log cannot
disagree.

### Two deliberate omissions

**Outcomes are not stored.** They are derived later by settling `strike`/`premium` against
`historical_prices` at expiration, exactly as the study scripts already do. Storing a computed
outcome would freeze it against a price series that a later split repair rewrites — the failure
mode already recorded in CLAUDE.md, where four CRWD rows manufactured −$168,516 of fiction.

**`spot` IS stored**, because the split scale guard needs the underlying price as of the pick date,
and that is the one input a repair makes unrecoverable.

### Safety

Instrumentation must never break the thing it instruments. `saveBriefCandidates` wraps everything
and returns 0 on failure rather than throwing; a dropped table logs a warning and the brief
completes normally. Covered by a test that drops the table mid-run.

### What this unlocks, and when

Nothing retroactively — the first rows appear on the next brief run. After roughly a month there
will be enough to ask whether the top-5 cut selects well, and after a quarter to ask it across
more than one regime. That is the question F8 leaves open and the only one in the wheel path that
currently has no path to an answer.
