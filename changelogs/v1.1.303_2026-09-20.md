# v1.1.303 — 2026-09-20

TradeAnalyzer only. Instrumentation for BPS-3 — no rule changed.

## The reframing

BPS-3 was written as a threshold question: the brief's liquidity bar is **5 of 15**, the v2 engine's
tradability bar is **8**, a spread pays four fills where a CSP pays two, so should the spread's pool
demand more? Reading the code moved the question.

`STRATEGY_RELEVANT_SLUGS.wheel` is `['short-naked-put', 'covered-call']`. **The gate that admits a ticker
to the spread's pool scores a one-leg structure**, and `strategy-lab-service.ts` sums its three components
*per leg and divides by `n`*. So the number is a statement about one near-the-money short put. The
spread's long wing — further OTM, thinner, wider, lower open interest, and the leg most likely to be
illiquid — is not in it at all.

It is not the threshold that is wrong. It is the structure being measured.

Two things reinforce that. `STRATEGY_RELEVANT_SLUGS.inflection` is `['short-put-vertical', …]`, so the Lab
**already computes** a both-leg liquidity score for the exact structure the spread trades — it is simply
wired to a different strategy's gate. And the spread already carries a direct measurement of the same
quantity: the round-trip cost share of the two contracts it would actually trade, computed from captured
bid/ask, which is the best-evidenced rule in the whole #43 study.

## Why the bar was not simply raised to 8

Every measured fact points away from it:

- The 8 bar **sits in the densest part of the distribution** — across 250 stored wheel picks the mode is
  exactly 7, one point below.
- **31% have a tradable ≤15% spread and are rejected as illiquid anyway.**
- **37% flip on the volume component alone**, and volume is cumulative within the session, so the ~10:15
  run scores lower than the ~17:00 run on an identical contract. The spread runs at both slots, so the
  bar would mean two different things in one day.
- **Liquidity is a risk proxy, confirmed twice** — the liquid subset *underperforms*. A higher bar can
  select names that are moving.

So the decision was to measure before changing anything.

## What was added

`liquidityVsCost()` in `spread-mode-settle.ts`, shown in the report and the 🪜 Spread Shadow view:
Spearman rank correlation (ranks, so one wide quote cannot set the answer) plus four score bands with
median cost share and a count over the 25% gate.

Two details that decide whether the check is worth anything:

- **Cost rejections are included.** A rejection row stores nulls — the cost share it was computed from
  survives only inside its sentence (`round-trip cost 64% of credit`). `parseCostShareFromReason` recovers
  it. Judging a liquidity proxy on accepted picks alone would be judging it on rows that already passed
  the cost gate, which removes exactly the expensive half of the distribution the check is about.
- **The check can come out both ways.** Pinned by two tests — one fixture where more liquid really is
  cheaper (Spearman < −0.9) and one where cost is flat across the score (|Spearman| < 0.3). A check that
  can only report "flat" is decoration, the shape of the conviction band that could never fire.

## The limitation, stated in the output

**Censored by construction.** Everything in the pool already cleared the bar, so no score below it is
observable. This can say whether the score discriminates *where it operates*; it cannot say what a lower
bar would admit. The report prints that line every time rather than leaving the reader to infer it, and
below three pairs it says "too few to say anything" rather than showing a number — the QC-907 shape again,
where no signal and a weak signal must not look alike.

The `premium` pool has no `brief_candidates` row and so no score to join; those rows are counted as
`unpaired` and reported, not dropped.

## What decides BPS-3

If the relationship is **flat**, the gate is measuring the wrong structure and no threshold fixes it — the
options then are the vertical's own score (already computed) or dropping the upstream bar and relying on
the cost gate. If it **tracks**, the bar is doing real work for the spread and the threshold becomes a
fair question again.

n is thin today — 10 positions plus the cost rejections — and triples from Monday with three arms.
