# v1.1.142 — 2026-09-01

## Validation — hold-period (`hold_days`) sweep REJECTED: the effect is regime, not edge

No code change. Recording a null finding so it is not re-litigated.

### The hypothesis

From the 2026-08-28 hand-run P&L study: brief picks looked positive over ~5 days and
negative over ~27, in two seemingly independent tests. In that study the engine's own exit
rules barely mattered — the 2× stop fired 16 times for −$52k and the 50% target 73 times for
+$40k, while **85 positions bled out on defensive-DTE for −$95k**. Hold length looked like
the dominant lever.

Brief Replay already stores `ret_5d` / `ret_10d` / `ret_21d` per pick, so the sweep needed no
new machinery.

### A one-month preliminary read looked decisive and was wrong

June 2026 alone (516 picks, pre-repair prices) showed clean monotone decay and a tidy
strategy story. Six months reverses it:

| median alpha | 5d | 10d | 21d | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| income (June) | +2.27 | +2.28 | +4.19 | "improves with time" |
| income (6 mo) | −0.08 | −0.28 | −1.30 | decays |
| buy (June) | −1.04 | −2.68 | −13.52 | "badly anti-predictive" |
| buy (6 mo) | +0.79 | +0.91 | **+2.38** | best performer, improves |

June is the single most pro-short-hold month in the whole sample — and it was the month the
preliminary read used.

### Six-month result

Range **2026-02-02 → 2026-07-31**: 125 trading days, 750 replay briefs, **3,189 picks**, run
*after* the v1.1.139–141 split and ticker-reuse repairs so the price series is clean. Returns
are direction-adjusted and SPY-relative; the test is **paired** (same pick at both horizons),
so cross-sectional dispersion cancels.

```
alpha(5d) − alpha(21d) = −1.01%,  t = −3.18
```

Significant — with the **wrong sign**. Over the full window, 21 days beat 5.

| month | diff | t | winner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-02 | −2.15% | −4.44 | 21d |
| 2026-03 | −0.55% | −0.96 | 21d |
| 2026-04 | −6.66% | −5.57 | 21d |
| 2026-05 | −5.10% | −5.18 | 21d |
| 2026-06 | **+6.99%** | **+13.00** | 5d |
| 2026-07 | +1.16% | +2.81 | 5d |

**The sign flips by month and every month is individually "significant."** 5d wins 2 of 6.
Per-strategy is equally split: wheel +1.52 (t 3.50) and inflection +1.99 (t 2.37) favour
short holds; bullish −3.11 (t −3.51) and buy −5.59 (t −6.32) favour long; income is flat
(t 0.93).

### Verdict

**REJECTED — `hold_days` stays 5, unchanged.** FR-SEY-5 requires a lever to be at-worst-neutral
on the historical sample. A sign-unstable effect means any fitted value is fitted to whichever
month was sampled. Same disposition as the SPY-regime gate, the overbought guard and FR-SEY-3.

### Process lesson, recorded deliberately

**One month cannot establish a duration or regime effect, however monotone it looks.** The June
read was monotone across five of six strategies and still inverted on a larger sample. Monotonicity
was the criterion used to *accept* signal in the `optionLiquidity` sweep (non-monotone → noise);
it is necessary, not sufficient — a single regime can be monotone and meaningless.

### What survives — structural, not a lever

Mean alpha *rises* with horizon (+0.69 → +1.05 → +1.70) while median *falls* (+0.32 → +0.01 →
−1.01). The typical pick decays; a few large winners carry the tail. Feb–Jul 2026 was a
semiconductor/memory melt-up — INTC 41→133, MU 322→1214, SNDK 573→2335, all verified as genuine
sustained rallies (max single-day 19–26%, no discontinuities) rather than data artifacts.

So a long hold is a **positive-skew bet on a trending regime**: it pays there and bleeds
otherwise. That also explains why the August hand-run study, covering a single quarter, read the
opposite way. Any future work here should target *regime detection*, not a fixed hold length.
