# v1.1.128 — 2026-08-05

## Wheel expiry selection: monthly-first (`WHEEL_MONTHLY_FIRST_EXPIRY`, default OFF)

Root-cause fix for the `optionLiquidity` signal reading **0/10 across ~94% of the universe**,
including WMT, PG, DIS and JPM. Ships **disabled** pending forward validation (FR-SEY-5).

### The investigation

v1.1.127 fixed the *reporting* of unmeasured liquidity (null, not a fabricated 0) but explicitly
did not explain why tickers that **did** resolve a chain still scored 0. Sampling the 2026-08-03
snapshot:

| ticker | `optionLiquidityScore` |
|---|---|
| PG, DIS | 0 / 10 |
| WMT | 2 / 10 |
| JPM, KO, V | 4 / 10 |
| AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, AMZN, GOOGL | 6 / 10 |
| TSLA | 10 / 10 |

Those are among the deepest options markets in existence. The first hypothesis — bad quote data
in the snapshot job — was **wrong**. The real cause is which contract gets measured.

### Root cause

`findSuitableChain` walks expirations in **ascending date order and returns the first one** that
yields a delta-band match. It never compares candidates. Since `getNearTermExpirations` generates
consecutive Fridays, **the earliest qualifying expiry is structurally a weekly.**

The `[30,45]` DTE window compounds it. Consecutive 3rd Fridays are 28–35 days apart, so a 16-day
window puts a monthly in range only ~53% of days. From 2026-08-03:

```
qualifying window [30,45] DTE:
  2026-09-04  32 DTE   weekly    ← first by date, wins
  2026-09-11  39 DTE   weekly
  2026-09-18  46 DTE   MONTHLY   ← clipped by ONE day
```

Every row in that 1,029-ticker snapshot landed on Sep 4.

### Why that destroys the signal

Open interest at the same ~25 delta, 2026-08-05, Sep-4 weekly vs Sep-18 monthly:

| ticker | weekly | monthly | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| PG | **7** | 4,063 | 580× |
| V | 15 | 802 | 53× |
| DIS | 56 | 2,982 | 53× |
| JPM | 65 | 879 | 14× |
| KO | 84 | 1,702 | 20× |
| WMT | 109 | 13,824 | **127×** |
| GOOGL | 259 | 1,757 | 7× |
| AAPL | 450 | 7,668 | 17× |

PG's Sep-4 $142 put has **open interest 7** — of course it scores 0. PG's monthly has 4,063.

So `optionLiquidityScore` measures a contract nobody would wheel and charges the **ticker** for it.
The signal cannot separate a mega-cap from a microcap; it is effectively a constant contributing
its full weight (wheel 9, income 13) as a near-uniform penalty across the candidate pool.

### The change (enabled)

- Try **3rd-Friday monthlies first**, then everything else ascending.
- Widen the window `[30,45]` → `[28,52]`.
- **No extra API calls** — still one chain fetch in the common case, just a different try-order.
  (The alternative considered, fetching every qualifying expiry and picking the highest-OI
  contract, would turn 1 fetch into 2–3 across ~1,029 tickers on a job that already runs 25–30
  minutes. Rejected on cost for a signal that only needs to be roughly right.)

The widening deliberately does **not** guarantee a monthly is always in range. A guarantee needs a
~35-day-wide window (e.g. `[28,63]`) and a 63-DTE CSP sits outside the wheel's premium-selling
tenor. `[28,52]` takes coverage from ~53% to ~82% of days; on the rest the nearest weekly is used
exactly as today. Ordering does most of the work regardless — even inside the *old* window the
monthly lost whenever a weekly happened to be nearer.

### Why it ships disabled

It changes the **recommended CSP itself**, not just the liquidity score:

- `premiumYield` (wheel weight 19) moves with the new strike/expiry/premium.
- Stage-4 pool selection and final picks move with the composite.
- `options_premium_history` — accumulating since v0.32.94 toward the deferred real income/wheel
  ROC analysis — gets a discontinuity when its basis shifts from weeklies to monthlies.

And a clean retrospective A/B is **not possible**: the `analysis_snapshots` panel (66 wheel
snapshots, 2026-05-14 → 2026-08-03) holds the *old* measurements, and recomputing the new ones
historically needs option chains that were never stored. Validation must therefore be
forward-looking — run with `WHEEL_MONTHLY_FIRST=1` during market hours and compare. Same
ship-disabled precedent as `ETF_AWARE_WHEEL_SCORING` (v1.1.118).

### This is NOT the lever rejected yesterday

v1.1.126 tested preferring monthlies in `selectExpiration` (`strategy-lab-service.ts`,
closest-to-30 within `[20,50]`) for the structures the **engine trades**, and rejected it: the
engine's `liquidityScore ≥ 8` gate already screens thin weeklies out, so the friction saving is
never paid, and a monthly moved off ~30 DTE is thinner than the weekly it replaces.

This is a different code path (`findSuitableChain`, first-match within `[30,45]`) serving a
different purpose: characterising a **ticker for scoring**, not choosing a trade. The rejected
finding does not argue against this one.
