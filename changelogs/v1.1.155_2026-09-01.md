# v1.1.155 — 2026-09-01

## fix: LONG options were priced as short premium, inverting their sign

### How it was found

By reconciling against E*Trade's own **Gains & Losses** export. The app's realized total read
**$3,309.67** against E*Trade's **$2,457.50** — and the operator's instinct, *"probably you are
not seeing losses"*, was exactly right.

### The bug

`matchClosedPositions` computed profit as `(open.price − close.price) × multiplier` for **every**
option. That is only correct for short premium. It also labelled every put `CSP` and every call
`CC` regardless of direction.

```
TSLA Jul 17 '26 $375 Put
  E*Trade:  BUY 19.63 (06/10) → SELL 15.98 (06/25)  =  −$364.06   Long Put
  recorded:                                            +$361.97   "CSP"
```

**A $726 error on a single row, in the direction that flatters the book** — which is why only the
broker's own statement surfaced it.

### The fix

`TxLeg` now carries `openedShort`, set from the order's `orderAction` (`SELL_OPEN` → short,
`BUY_OPEN` → long):

- **P&L follows direction** — short profits when the close is cheaper, long when it is dearer
- **Positions are labelled correctly** — `Long Put` / `Long Call`, matching the labels the
  position sync already writes, instead of being mislabelled as wheel positions
- **`premium_received` is null for a long option** — it pays a debit, it does not collect premium

The transactions path can only ever infer `open` from a SELL, so its opening legs are short by
construction. That is one more reason orders is the primary source: it cannot represent a long
option at all.

### Reconciliation against the export

**22 of 24 rows now agree to the cent.** The two remaining differences are both understood:

| row | app | E*Trade | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TSLA $350 put (assigned) | +$499.48 | *absent* | an assignment **defers** the premium into the stock's cost basis rather than realising it — a tax-convention difference, not an error |
| NFLX $85 call | −$113.51 | −$137.03 | was still a last-mark estimate before this import |

The assignment difference is worth keeping in mind whenever the app's realized figure is compared
with a broker tax statement: the app records the premium as realized when the option closes, and
E*Trade holds it until the assigned shares are sold.
