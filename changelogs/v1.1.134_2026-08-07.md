# v1.1.134 — 2026-08-07

## Wheel (Vetted) thresholds reviewed after Q2 earnings — NO change

The review deferred from 2026-07-02, when the S&P 500 + Russell 1000 match count collapsed to
**~4** and the call was made *not* to re-tune because the thinness looked seasonal. That call was
correct, and this run closes it out.

### The count recovered on unchanged thresholds

| date | count | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-07-02 | **~4** | target expiry 2026-08-21, spanning Q2 season |
| 2026-08-07 | **73** | 58 stocks + 15 ETFs, target expiry 2026-09-18 |

No threshold moved between those two readings. The July thinness was the earnings gate doing its
job, and the list refilled by itself as names reported and their next date rolled past the target
expiry.

### Gate margins say nothing needs tuning

Distance to each bar among the **58 stock** survivors (ETFs excluded — they run a different path):

| gate | bar | min | p50 | within 10% of bar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| suitability ≥ 75 | 75 | 75.0 | 88.0 | 15/58 |
| open interest ≥ 250 | 250 | 262 | 1,682 | **1/58** |
| spread % ≤ 15 | 15 | 1.5 | 8.3 | 5/58 |
| annualized % ≥ 15 | 15 | **16.5** | 24.4 | **0/58** |
| IV % ≤ 80 | 80 | 21.9 | 36.8 | 3/58 |

The premium floor is not binding at all — **no stock survivor sits within 10% of the 15% bar**, the
lowest is 16.5%. The open-interest floor has a single name near it against a median of 1,682. So
loosening either would not add names; they are not the constraint. What binds is **suitability ≥75**
(by design — it is the quality bar) and **spread ≤15%**.

*Honest limit: margins among survivors describe the boundary, not the rejection volume. Without a
log of rejects this cannot say how many names each gate turns away upstream.*

### The 15 sub-threshold survivors are ETFs, not a leak

A suitability of **4** (IWM) or an **8.6%** yield (SPY) sitting in a list gated at ≥75 and ≥15%
reads like a broken filter. All 15 are ETFs on the documented ETF path
(`minAnnualizedReturnEtf: 8`, suitability bypassed because ETFs carry no fundamentals). Verified
against `constituents` rather than assumed.

### New finding — this is a quarterly cycle, and the next squeeze is worse

The earnings gate excludes any name reporting on/before the target monthly expiry, and the target is
the nearest 3rd Friday **≥25 DTE**. Four times a year that window lands squarely on an earnings
season. Share of the universe excluded by the earnings gate alone:

```
as-of        target expiry   excluded
2026-08-07   2026-09-18       16%   ##########
2026-08-21   2026-09-18        7%   ####
2026-09-18   2026-10-16        6%   ####
2026-09-25   2026-11-20       80%   ################################################
2026-10-16   2026-11-20       75%   #############################################
2026-10-23   2026-11-20       59%   ####################################
2026-11-06   2026-12-18        3%   ##
```

So **2026-07-02's ~4 and 2026-08-07's 73 are the same gates at opposite ends of one cycle**, and the
next collapse is due **~2026-09-25 → 2026-10-23** at roughly **80% excluded** — more severe than
July's, because the target expiry jumps straight from 2026-10-16 to 2026-11-20 and spans the whole
Q3 season (535 names report in October alone).

The list self-heals both times. **Do not tune against either extreme**: loosening during a trough
balloons the list once earnings clear — the same "don't re-engineer on a regime-specific sample"
lesson as the scoring-replay work. The cleanest read of any quarter is the 6–7% trough (here,
2026-08-21 → 2026-09-18).

This is arguably a feature: the gate implicitly enforces post-earnings entry, which is the right
discipline for a wheel. Whether it should instead roll a blocked name to the *next* monthly rather
than dropping it is a separate design question, deliberately not taken here.

### Correction recorded

An initial attempt to compute what the 2026-07-02 run faced produced a plausible-looking "116 names
excluded" — and it was **invalid**. `earnings_calendar` stores only each ticker's **next** report
date (nearest-wins, v1.1.75), so today's table cannot reconstruct a past run's state: every name
that has since reported now shows its Q3 date. The number was discarded rather than published. Far-
horizon rows in the projection above are a floor for the same reason.
