### The endpoint was never the only problem

`classifyTransaction` must infer intent from *"sold means opened, bought means closed"* and
then pairs opens to closes **by index**. On this account that yields nothing, and even when it
matches it mislabels a long-option round trip (`BUY_OPEN` → `SELL_CLOSE`) and an assignment.

Orders needs no inference at all:

- `orderAction` states OPEN vs CLOSE outright
- `averageExecutionPrice` is the realised fill

### Head to head, same account, same run

| source | HTTP | closed positions produced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| executed orders | 200 in 376 ms | **25, with real fills** |
| transactions | 200 on all 9 slices | **0** |

So the precedence is reversed: **orders primary, transactions fallback**. Transactions is kept
only because it is the documented alternative and may cover activity orders misses — dividends,
fees, corporate actions.

### Why v1.1.151's supersede appeared not to work

It was never invoked. Transactions won the race, produced no positions, and
`insertClosedPosition` was therefore never called — so the 13 estimated rows sat untouched and
the count stayed at 41. With orders primary they are superseded on the next import.

Both fallback directions are reported rather than silent: `Executed orders returned no legs;
trying transactions` and `Executed orders unavailable, falling back to transactions: …`.
