# v1.1.225 — 2026-09-11

## A scheduled job waiting for the mutex defers quietly

**Symptom.** A stream of identical toasts: *"Batch job 'batch-orchestrator' is already running.
'data-collector' was not started."*

**Why.** `BatchService.checkSchedule` ticks every 5 minutes. `data-collector` is due from 17:05 and
stays due until it succeeds (`last_success_date !== today`), so every tick tried to start it, found
the orchestrator holding the one-job-at-a-time mutex, refused — and raised a warning notification.
The after-close chain runs for hours: on 2026-09-10 the collector did not start until 19:33, which is
about thirty identical warnings.

**Why none of them mattered.**

1. The refusal is self-correcting — the next tick retries, and that is the designed behaviour of the
   17:05 slot (it is deliberately scheduled *after* this app's after-close chain begins).
2. `getSubJobs` returns **every enabled job**, so `data-collector` is also a member of the chain that
   holds the lock. It runs in the chain regardless of whether its own trigger ever wins.

So the notification reported a routine wait as a warning, once per tick. That is the same shape as the
70 gate emails migration 080 was written for: **an alarm that repeats while nothing is wrong teaches
the operator to ignore the channel.**

### Change

- A `schedule` trigger that finds the mutex held now logs `[batch] "<job>" deferred — "<holder>" holds
  the mutex; retrying on the next tick` and returns.
- `manual` and `startup` triggers keep the notification: there somebody is waiting for the job, and
  silence would be the bug.
- Nothing else changes — no `batch_runs` row is written for a refusal (as before), and both collector
  triggers stay: the standalone 17:05 covers a day the chain never runs, and the 19:30 watchdog
  catches a day the wait swallowed.

### Not changed, deliberately

`data-collector` having two triggers (its own schedule and chain membership) is redundant but correct,
and the redundancy is the point: either path alone would leave a hole on a day the other does not
happen.
