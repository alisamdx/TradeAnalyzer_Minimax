# v1.1.261 — 2026-09-17

## A ruled ticker reuse now ends the previous occupant's series

`recordTickerReuse` closes one membership range and opens another, and its own
documentation says why: *"so a reader intersecting bars with membership sees one entity
at a time."*

**There was no such reader.** QC-204, QC-205, QC-209 and QC-210 all select from
`raw_daily_bar` by ticker with no membership intersection, so a ruled reuse kept firing on
the previous occupant's bars on the very next run — the gate reopens a resolved exception
whose fingerprint fires again, by design, as a recurrence. An operator ruling on SOLS
would have lasted exactly until the next nightly run, and the 26 findings it was meant to
settle would have come back.

This was found while trying to rule the 26 pre-listing QC-204 findings from 2026-09-17,
and it is the reason none of them were ruled: the ruling had no way to stick.

## `entityStartDates()` — the narrow reading, and why it must be narrow

The new helper returns, per ticker, the `valid_from` of the open membership range whose
`change_reason` is `ticker_change`. Only `recordTickerReuse` writes such a row.

The obvious implementation — intersect bars with membership generally — is a trap, and it
has its own test. An ordinary `valid_from` is when the ticker joined **our universe
list** (2026-09-11 for every ticker in gen-002), not when the listing began. Reading it as
an entity boundary would discard every bar before the seed and gut all four checks at
once, while looking like a tightening.

The four readers:

- **QC-204** skips a comparison whose earlier bar precedes the splice — it would otherwise
  compare two companies.
- **QC-205** and **QC-209** drop pre-splice bars from `latestSeries`, so neither window
  reaches back across it.
- **QC-210** drops the ticker from its population entirely: a ruled splice is the answer
  to the question that check asks.

**Rehearsed on 2026-09-17: `Checks that changed: none`** — QC-204 151, QC-205 12, QC-209
13, QC-210 11, all identical. gen-002 holds no `ticker_change` row yet, so the change is
inert until an operator rules one. That is the result to want from this kind of change.

## The two rulings this unblocks

Both splices are corroborated by three independent signals — `list_date`, the largest step
in the series, and QC-209's own key:

```
SOLS   last old bar 2025-04-09  $0.0001  vol     1,500
       first new bar 2025-10-30  $48.74  vol 23,637,969     list_date 2025-10-20
JAN    last old bar 2024-07-12  $2.23    vol   139,578
       first new bar 2026-03-20  $23.60  vol 15,894,518     list_date 2026-03-20
```

```bash
node packages/core/scripts/exception.mjs reuse <id> SOLS 2025-10-30 "Solstice Advanced Materials listed into the shell; pre-2025-10-30 bars are the previous occupant"
node packages/core/scripts/exception.mjs reuse <id> JAN  2026-03-20 "Janus Living listed into the symbol; pre-2026-03-20 bars are the previous occupant"
```

**Pass the splice date explicitly — do not let it be inferred.** `inferSpliceDate` takes
the date from `ORDER BY last_seen_run_id DESC LIMIT 1` over the ticker's QC-204 findings,
and every one of SOLS's 21 shares run 19, so the tie is broken arbitrarily. For JAN it is
worse than arbitrary: six of its seven findings are **2022–2024** dates inside the previous
occupant's history, so the inference would close membership years early. The function's
own comment warns that getting this wrong is silent; on a ticker with more than one
finding — which is every reuse — the default is unsafe. Recorded here rather than changed,
because the fix belongs with whoever next touches that function and wants a test for it.
