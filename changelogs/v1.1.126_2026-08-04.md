# v1.1.126 — 2026-08-04

## Expiry-selection lever: validated and REJECTED (FR-SEY-5)

No behavior change. This release records a negative validation result and the evidence behind it.

### Background

`Requirements/NEW_FRONTIER.md §12` (2026-07-01) established that `selectExpiration`
(`src/main/services/strategy-lab-service.ts`) takes the expiration in **[20,50] DTE closest to 30
with no liquidity awareness**, and therefore lands on a thin **weekly** ~54% of the time. Standard
monthlies (3rd Friday) carry far more open interest and tighter spreads. A same-instant live A/B on
MRVL found the chosen weekly **40–160× thinner in OI and 2–4× wider in spread** than either
flanking monthly. §12 called this the largest identified lever on the "1 critic pass out of 1000+"
complaint — larger than structure selection (falsified, §11) or after-close measurement staleness
(§11, still open).

It was **not** shipped at the time because snapping a structure from its 30-DTE weekly to the
nearest monthly *changes the DTE* (on 2026-07-01 the monthlies sat at 16 and 51 DTE against the
chosen 29), which changes credit/theta economics. Per FR-SEY-5 the acceptance test is **net realized
outcome**, not the liquidity improvement — and that required settled trades, which July structures
did not provide until Jul 17–31. This release runs that test.

### New: `scripts/fr-expiry-realized-ab.mjs`

Two stages, deliberately separated because they answer different questions and only one of them can
be answered from real quotes.

**Stage 1 — execution friction, real stored quotes, no model.** Matched design: pair each
`ticker|slug`'s monthly-landed observations against *its own* weekly-landed observations, holding
DTE inside 27–33. Matching on ticker+slug removes the composition confound (which names carry
weeklies at all); the DTE band removes the tenor confound. Friction follows the app's own fill model
(`options-fill.ts`, `SLIPPAGE_FRACTION = 0.5` → per-leg cost `0.5 × (ask−bid)/2`, every leg).

**Stage 2 — realized P&L of the tenor shift, 12-month simulated panel.** The stored-brief sample
spans only 24 settled entry dates in one regime and holds no chain for the counterfactual monthly,
so the tenor question cannot be answered from it. Stage 2 replicates
`BacktestEngine.simulateSingleSpread` — the same replica the budget-width / wheel / iron-condor
FR-SEY studies used — with both arms priced by the same Black-Scholes model off `iv_history` ATM IV
on the same (ticker, entry date) panel, so model bias cancels and the delta is pure tenor.
Settlement is real expiry intrinsic from `historical_prices`.

### Results

**Stage 1 — the liquidity gap is real and large** (871 matched cells, 31 tickers, 29 structure
types, DTE held at 27–33):

| measure | monthly | weekly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| friction, % of credit collected | **19.6%** | **40.3%** |
| open interest / leg | 1,569 | 83 |
| `liquidityScore` (0–15) | 8.4 | 4.7 |

Monthly is cheaper on **30/31 tickers** (each ticker one vote).

**But two findings kill the lever:**

1. **The friction saving is never actually paid.** The engine's tradability gate
   (`liquidityScore ≥ 8`, enforced by `EntryPolicy`) already rejects the thin weeklies. Restricted
   to engine structures that *clear the gate* — the population the engine really trades — friction
   is **monthly 6.9% vs weekly 6.0% of premium**: indistinguishable, and if anything the weekly is
   marginally cheaper. The entire 20-point aggregate gap lives inside structures that never trade.

2. **The monthly's OI advantage is concentrated at 23–33 DTE — exactly where the current rule
   already picks it.** Gate-clear rate by DTE bucket:

   | bucket | monthly | weekly |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 14–22 DTE | 23% (n=590) | — |
   | 23–33 DTE | **45%** (n=5,384) | 24% (n=10,746) |
   | 34–44 DTE | 20% (n=2,705) | — |
   | 45–55 DTE | 18% (n=947) | — |

   A monthly pulled *away* from ~30 DTE — the only case where the rule currently chooses a weekly —
   lands on a contract **thinner than the weekly it replaced**. The headline "24% → 51%" deployment
   gain is an artifact of measuring monthlies only at their best tenor; DTE-weighted it is
   **28% → 30–31%**.

**Stage 2 — realized P&L.** 83 weekly-carrying brief tickers × 213 entry dates = **17,679 paired
(ticker, date) cells**, 2025-08-01 → 2026-06-05. Short put vertical, 0.30Δ short + budget wing
(≤$500 max loss), 50% profit target / 200% stop / else expiry.

| policy | Δgross vs current | Δnet vs current | DTE-weighted gate-clear × net | expected pts / candidate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **current (today)** | — | — | 28% × 1.53% | **0.533** |
| `monthly[23,33]` (narrow tie-break) | −0.09 | −0.14 | 31% × 1.39% | 0.462 |
| `monthly[20,50]` (in-band tie-break) | +0.15 | −0.42 | 30% × 1.11% | 0.384 |
| `monthly[14,55]` (widened band) | −0.41 | −0.85 | 30% × 0.68% | 0.282 |

The gross (tenor) effect is **noise** — it flips sign month to month, from −2.10 to +2.51 pts — so
the DTE shift is neither the win nor the loss. But no variant is better, and every variant is worse
once friction and DTE-weighted deployment are applied. `monthly[23,33]`, the best-case narrow
variant constructed specifically so tenor is never dragged into the thin 34–55 range, is closest to
neutral and still does not beat doing nothing.

### Resolution

**No code change, no config knob.** Same discipline as the SPY-regime gate, the overbought guard,
and FR-SEY-3: a rule that cannot be shown to help on the data does not ship. It is not shipped
*disabled* either — that would add a lever with a known-negative expectation.

§12's **liquidity** claim stands and remains correct. What fails is the inference that acting on it
improves outcomes: the binding constraint is the `liquidityScore ≥ 8` entry gate, not the expiry
rule. The residual low-pass-rate complaint is therefore **not** addressable via expiry selection.

### Methodology note (worth keeping)

A naive **pooled** weekly-vs-monthly comparison **inverts** the Stage-1 friction result — pooled, the
weekly looks marginally *cheaper* as a fraction of credit (15.0% vs 15.6% on short put verticals) —
because monthly-landed rows are dominated by names that carry no weeklies at all, observed at off-30
DTE, at different price levels. Matching on ticker+slug and holding DTE fixed is load-bearing, not
cosmetic. Any future re-run of this question must keep the matched design.
