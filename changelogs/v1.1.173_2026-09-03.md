# v1.1.173 — 2026-09-03

## T2 — a wheel composite floor is REJECTED. The composite is anti-predictive at the top.

No behaviour change. `MIN_COMPOSITE_BY_STRATEGY` still gates bearish at 70 and nothing else.

### The question

Bearish earned a 70 floor from a full-year replay showing sub-70 picks carry ~0 alpha and lose
money. Wheel has never been tested for an equivalent, so on a weak day the dice pick can be
whatever happened to be least-bad. This tests whether a floor would help.

### Why replay could not be used

`brief-replay-service.ts` is explicit: replay is blind to `premiumYield` (19 of wheel's 91 weight),
which shifts and compresses the composite scale, so "never carry an absolute composite threshold
between the two for wheel/income." A gate *is* an absolute threshold. (Bearish's 70 was safely
replay-derived precisely because bearish has no options-derived signal.)

So this runs on **live `daily_briefs` wheel picks**, settling each stored CSP at intrinsic against
the real close on its expiration. `scripts/fr-wheel-composite-floor-study.mjs`.

**180 settled live picks · 63 tickers · 35 brief dates → 101 independent episodes** (ticker +
expiry). The mandatory scale guard excluded **0** rows — stored legs agree with the price series.

### The answer: the relationship runs backwards

| composite | n | mean return | median | assigned | losers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–58 | 5 | +0.69% | +1.99% | 20.0% | 20% |
| 58–63 | 43 | **+1.91%** | +2.44% | 16.3% | 7% |
| 63–68 | 27 | +1.84% | +2.64% | 22.2% | 19% |
| 68–73 | 13 | −1.08% | −0.75% | 61.5% | 54% |
| 73+ | 13 | **−4.99%** | +4.72% | 50.6% | 46% |

Every candidate threshold makes the surviving book **worse**, monotonically, while emptying days:

| gate | kept | kept return | Δ vs no gate | days emptied |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 101 | +0.56% | — | 0/35 |
| 62 | 57 | −0.35% | −0.91 | 7/35 |
| 65 | 38 | −1.13% | −1.69 | 8/35 |
| 68 | 26 | −3.03% | −3.59 | 13/35 |
| 70 | 19 | −3.89% | −4.45 | **14/35** |

**Not outlier-driven.** Trimming both tails leaves the sign and the gap intact, and the medians —
immune to the blow-ups — agree:

| | mean | trimmed 3/tail | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥68 | −3.03% | −1.84% | **+0.27%** |
| <68 | +1.80% | +2.32% | **+2.44%** |

### The mechanism

The worst episodes name it: **MRVL ×3** (−22.2%, −16.4%, −16.4%), **RKLB ×2** (−24.5%, −14.2%),
**COHR ×2**, **ON** — high-IV semiconductor and space names. The wheel composite's two heaviest
signals are `ivRank` (22) and `premiumYield` (19) — **41 of 91 weight**, both rewarding premium
richness. Premium richness is *compensation for risk*, not evidence of edge. Selecting harder on
the composite therefore selects for higher implied volatility, which delivers more assignment
(61.5% in the 68–73 band vs 16.3% in 58–63) and worse realised returns.

This is the same shape as v1.1.170's liquidity finding, where liquidity tracked attention which
tracked names that were moving. **Twice now, a wheel signal that looks like quality has turned out
to be a risk proxy.**

### Recorded, not acted on

The tempting inference — invert or cap the composite for wheel — is **not** taken. Precedent is
explicit: buy/bullish anti-predictiveness was found on a similar sample and recorded as
"weak/regime-bound — don't re-engineer on this sample." Same caution applies. n=13 per top band,
one regime, held to expiry with no roll or post-assignment covered call (all of which cushion
exactly the assigned cases concentrated in the high-IV bands).

**Highest-value follow-up, flagged not built:** Roll the Dice breaks ties by **highest composite
score**. If a high wheel composite is anti-predictive, that tiebreak may be selecting the worst of
the eligible set rather than the best. The dice pool is small (≤5) and sits behind the critic,
liquidity and earnings gates, so this does not follow automatically — but it is the single most
decision-relevant open question in the wheel path and deserves its own study once more picks have
settled.
