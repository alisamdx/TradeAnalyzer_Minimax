# v1.1.131 — 2026-08-06

## `WHEEL_GRADED_LIQUIDITY` — continuous wheel liquidity scoring (default OFF)

The follow-on to v1.1.130, which established that fixing **which** contract gets measured
(`WHEEL_MONTHLY_FIRST_EXPIRY`) was necessary but not sufficient — the score still sat at a
near-constant 0 or 4. This fixes how the contract is **scored**.

### The defect

`optionLiquidityScore` is three binary gates:

```
OI     >= 50   -> +4     fires often
spread <= 5%   -> +4     almost never
volume >= 50   -> +2     rarely
```

A 5% *relative* bid-ask on a 25-delta put is institutional-grade tight. Against a panel whose
**median spread is 16.9%**, that gate is effectively unreachable — WMT with open interest 13,824
and a 5.2% spread misses it by 0.2pp. Per-strike volume ≥50 is similarly rare, and it accrues
through the session so it reads lower in the morning. The OI gate ends up carrying the whole
score.

### Calibration — real data, not a copied shape

New `scripts/fr-wheel-liquidity-calibration.mjs`.

**Data source note worth remembering:** the panel is mined from stored `daily_briefs` structures —
every short-put leg in the 0.20–0.30 delta band with a live bid, deduped by date+ticker+strike+
expiration. Those quotes were captured *during market hours* by the brief job across many dates, so
this needs **no live market** and yields far more observations than polling chains. (The first
attempt polled the API at 08:24 ET and collected zero rows — outside RTH every bid is 0. The script
now says so explicitly instead of reporting an empty result, and keeps `--source live` as an
option.)

**1,278 observations.** Ground truth = what the contract actually costs to trade under the app's own
fill model (`options-fill.ts`, `SLIPPAGE_FRACTION 0.5`): `0.5 × half-spread` as a share of the
credit collected. Panel medians: OI 74, spread 16.9%, volume 6, entry cost 4.4% of credit.

Schemes were judged on two axes — **discrimination** (a signal returning the same value for every
ticker contributes nothing regardless of its weight) and **agreement with real execution cost**:

| scheme | distinct values | % scoring 0 | entropy | Spearman(score, cost%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| legacy (binary gates) | 6 | 38% | 0.75 | −0.419 |
| Strategy-Lab tiers rescaled to 0–10 | 15 | 0% | 0.82 | −0.772 |
| retuned tiers | 18 | 11% | 0.89 | −0.751 |
| **continuous — adopted** | **21** | **2%** | **0.97** | **−0.799** |

The single clearest data point: the legacy scheme scores **KO** — spread 13.9%, volume 39, real
entry cost **3.6% of credit** — a flat **0.0/10**. A contract that is cheap to trade scores zero.

### Adopted formula

```
spread pts (max 4) = 4 × clamp01(1 − (spread% − 5) / 35)    // 5% → 4, 40% → 0
OI     pts (max 4) = 4 × clamp01(log10(max(OI, 1)) / 3.3)    // saturates ≈ 2,000 contracts
volume pts (max 2) = 2 × clamp01(log10(max(vol, 1)) / 2)     // saturates ≈ 100 contracts
```

No cliffs: under legacy, a 0.3pp spread move (5.2% → 4.9%) swings the score a full 4 points; under
graded it moves less than 0.2. Both behaviours are pinned by tests.

### Honest limit of the evidence

`cost%` is derived from the spread, so **any scheme that grades spread will beat one that gates it
on this metric near-tautologically.** The Spearman column shows graded beats binary *on the
component it measures*. It is **not** independent proof that the OI and volume weights are right —
those price exit optionality and depth, which a single-contract entry-cost measure cannot see. A
wheel CSP that has to be rolled needs a book on the other side, and nothing here measures that.

### Latent bug fixed in the graded branch

A **no-bid** quote (`bid = 0`) falls into the degenerate-quote path — written for *locked* quotes
(`bid === ask > 0`, e.g. the v3 snapshot's `day.close` proxy, where there genuinely is no spread to
measure) — and is handed **full spread credit**. That scores the worst possible liquidity as the
best. The graded branch gives it zero; the legacy branch is deliberately left unchanged, since it is
the shipped default, and its behaviour is pinned by a test so the difference is documented rather
than accidental. Unreachable through `findSuitableChain` (which filters `bid > 0`), but
`wheelOptionLiquidityScore` is exported.

### Why it stays OFF

The signal carries weight **9 (wheel) / 13 (income)**, so enabling it re-ranks candidates and
therefore changes what the brief surfaces and what the engine can trade. FR-SEY-5 wants realized
outcomes; a calibration against execution cost is not that.

**Independent of `WHEEL_MONTHLY_FIRST_EXPIRY`** — that fixes which contract is measured, this fixes
how it is scored. Either helps alone, and they are complementary: monthly-first moves the contract
to one with ~50× the open interest, graded scoring then actually registers the difference.
