# v1.1.174 — 2026-09-03

## The dice tiebreak is nearly vacuous — the F8 concern does not land where it was aimed

No behaviour change. `scripts/fr-dice-tiebreak-study.mjs` added.

### The question

v1.1.173 (T2) found the wheel composite anti-predictive at the top: mean return on capital ran
**+1.91%** in the 58–63 band down to **−4.99%** at 73+. Roll the Dice sorts its eligible set by
`compositeScore` and takes the highest, so the obvious worry was that the tiebreak selects the
worst survivor. That was recorded as the most decision-relevant open question in the wheel path.

### The answer is structural, not statistical

The tiebreak almost never has anything to break.

| eligible candidates | days |
|---|---|
| 0 | 28 |
| **1** | **27** |
| 2 | 6 |
| 3 | 1 |

Across 62 stored brief days: 34 produced a pick, and **on 27 of those 34 (79%) there was exactly
one eligible candidate**. Only **7 days** offered a real choice; only **4** are fully settled.

So whatever F8 implies about high composites, **the dice's exposure to it at selection time is
negligible.** The gates ahead of the sort — critic `pass` and non-degraded, liquidity ≥ 8,
earnings clear of expiry, real credit — do essentially all the work, leaving a single survivor.

Chosen **+2.02%** vs alternatives **+2.10%**; the chosen pick beat its own day's alternatives on

(2026-07-14 is the one day that looks like F8 in miniature — the highest composite assigned and
lost while a 61.6 returned +2.15% — but a single day is a story, not evidence.)

### Where the exposure actually is

The composite's real influence on the dice is **upstream**. The brief keeps only
`PICKS_PER_STRATEGY = 5` by composite out of a `STAGE4_POOL_SIZE = 15` pool, and the dice can only
ever see those 5. **That cut is where an anti-predictive composite would do its damage** — and it
is not testable from `daily_briefs`, which stores only the survivors.

`analysis_snapshots` is the closest thing available: **65 dates, ~1,223 wheel rows each (79,477
total)**, every row carrying a real `recommendedStrike`, `premium`, `delta`, `ivRank` and
`optionLiquidityScore`. It does **not** store `compositeScore`, and 21 of the composite's 91 weight
(`fundamentalScore` + `leverageQuality`) comes from `fundamentals_cache`, which keeps one row per
ticker with no history. So a **selection study on the reconstructable signals is possible; a
faithful composite replay is not.**

### Recorded as the cheap fix that would make it testable

Persisting the composite and the eventual outcome for **all 15** Stage-4 candidates — not just the
5 that survive — would make the top-5 cut directly measurable from then on. Small, additive, and
the only way this question becomes answerable rather than inferred. Logged in
`.ai/AI_CONTEXT.md` §11.
