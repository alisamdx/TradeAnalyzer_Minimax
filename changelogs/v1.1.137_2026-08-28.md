# v1.1.137 — 2026-08-28

## fix: let a complete sweep absorb a small scheduled-date slip

### Why

v1.1.136 forbade the Finnhub sweep from ever pushing a stored future earnings date
further out. That is correct for a ~90-day jump — the signature of a coverage gap hiding
the near date and returning the following quarter, the exact failure that put NVDA in the
brief on its earnings day — but it also refused the routine case of an issuer refining a
scheduled date by a day or two.

Measured on the live calendar immediately after the v1.1.136 job run (2026-08-28), against
a narrow 45-day Finnhub window (585 entries, far under the 1500 cap, so it cannot be
truncated and is ground truth):

| stored vs truth, 69 constituents reporting within 45d | count |
| --- | --- |
| exact match | 50 |
| stored **later** than truth (the dangerous direction) | **0** |
| stored earlier than truth (fail-safe but stale) | 19 |
| missing | 0 |

Zero rows stored late — v1.1.136 did its job. But the 19 early rows were 1–8 days stale
(ACN and SAIL by 8), and every one had been written in **June or July**: the sweep kept
seeing the corrected date, kept refusing it as a later move, and the row stayed frozen at
an old estimate until it went stale enough for Phase 2 to refetch.

An early date is fail-safe for the −20 penalty (it engages sooner, never later), so nothing
was at risk. It is simply imprecise, and an 8-day-early date excludes a name from
wheel/income for about a week longer than warranted.

### Change

A **complete** sweep may now move a stored future date later by at most
`MAX_LATER_SLIP_DAYS` (14). Beyond that it is still refused, as is any later move at all
from an incomplete sweep. Moving a date **nearer** remains unbounded — it is fail-safe and
is what heals poisoned rows. Anything refused is still corrected by Phase 2 once the stored
date goes stale.

14 days is comfortably above an issuer's typical one-or-two-day refinement and far below the
~90-day leap that signals a coverage gap.

### Validation (FR-SEY-5)

No threshold or rule changed: the 45-day penalty window and the −20 magnitude are untouched.
This moves the gate's *input data* toward ground truth, verified above against an
untruncatable Finnhub window.

A Brief Replay delta is not merely unhelpful here but **impossible**: `earnings_calendar`
holds only each ticker's NEXT date, one row per ticker, overwritten in place — there is no
history from which to reconstruct what any past run saw (already documented in
`.ai/AI_CONTEXT.md` §10 in the Wheel-Vetted threshold entry). A replay would re-read
today's corrected dates for both arms and show no difference by construction.

Note this makes the gate very slightly *less* conservative in one direction: a name whose
stored date was early may now no longer fall inside the 45-day window. That is the intended
correction — it was being penalised on a date the issuer had already moved.
