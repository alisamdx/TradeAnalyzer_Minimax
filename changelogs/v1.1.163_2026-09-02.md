# v1.1.163 — 2026-09-02

## feat: Roll the Dice by email — one opportunity, built as a card rather than a report

> *"happy for any suggestion to make it more user friendly since we do not need a grid to tell
> the story on 1 single opportunity"*

Correct — a grid is the wrong shape for one trade, because there is nothing to compare it
against. The brief's email is a grid because it carries ~25 picks across 5 strategies. This
carries one, so it is laid out as a card.

### Design decisions

**The subject line carries the entire trade.**

```
🎲 NVDA — sell the $205 put, Fri Sep 25 · $744 credit
🎲 No dice today — nothing cleared (1 considered)
```

This is the part most likely to be read — on a phone lock screen, without opening anything — so
the ticker, strike, expiry and credit all go in it rather than a generic "your pick is ready".

**Lead with the order, not the analysis.** The first thing in the body is *Sell 1 NVDA $205 put*,
then expiry and days. The scoring is already done; the email's job is to be actionable.

**Three numbers, then the question a wheel entry actually turns on.** You collect / cash required
/ **if assigned, you own it at $X (Y% below today's price)**. That last line is the real decision
— am I happy owning this here? — so it gets equal weight, not a footnote.

**The downside in plain language with real numbers**, not a disclaimer: *"If NVDA is below $205 on
Fri Sep 25 you buy 100 shares at $205, costing $20,500. Your break-even is $197.56 — below that
you are down roughly $100 for every dollar further it falls."*

**"Passed every gate" is spelled out** so the reader knows why they can act without reading, and
the rejections are listed so the bar is visible rather than claimed.

**A no-pick day still sends.** Silence cannot be distinguished from a failed job or a broken SMTP
config, and the point of the feature is that the operator can stop checking. The empty card says
so plainly and notes that ~2 days in 3 is normal, so it does not read as a malfunction.

No PDF attachment — one trade does not need one.

### Delivery

Sent from **inside the brief job**, right after the brief's own email, rather than as its own
batch step. It is a pure filter over the brief that job just persisted, so running it inline
guarantees the ordering and avoids adding a `sequence_order` row — a column `registerJob` seeds
only on fresh installs, which has now caused four separate defects (v1.1.121, v1.1.123,
migration 067, v1.1.160). Failure is caught and logged; it can never fail the batch.

Also an **✉ Email it** button on the in-app dice panel, and `scoring:email-roll-the-dice`. All
three routes go through the new `computeRollTheDice(db)`, so the email, the panel and the IPC
cannot disagree about what today's pick is — the same guarantee the brief email already makes by
building from persisted picks.

### Email HTML constraints

Table layout (Outlook ignores flex/grid), every style inline (Gmail strips `<style>`), explicit
light background and text colour on every cell (client dark-mode is applied inconsistently, and a
transparent background is how "invisible text in dark mode" happens), `max-width:560px` with
`width:100%` for phones.

### Note on the preview

Rendering the populated card from real data selected **NVDA from the 2026-08-26 brief — its actual
earnings day**. That is v1.1.162's documented blind spot showing up immediately: `earnings_calendar`
keeps one row per ticker with no history, so a historical replay sees today's next-report date and
the earnings gate cannot fire retrospectively. Live, that pick would have been rejected. The
preview is honest about what the code does with historical data; it is not what the gate does
today.

11 unit tests (subject content both ways, the three numbers, the plain-language risk line, HTML
escaping of model-authored critic text, single-column sizing, no `<style>` block). Suite: 1069.
