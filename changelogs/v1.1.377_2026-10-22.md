# v1.1.377 — 2026-10-22

## TradeAnalyzer 2: QC-705 no longer judges filings waiting to be re-parsed

`core` 0.6.85, `next` 0.4.70.

### What was firing

QC-705 compares each report filing's own XBRL instance with SEC companyfacts, using only the filing's newest instance read. Parser revision 2 (v1.1.371) fixed two faults: it kept rounded duplicate facts, and it keyed one-day durations as ranges. Filings read under revision 1 are re-read by `filingFactsRereadQueue`, which gets budget only after new filings.

On 2026-10-22, run 104, QC-705 had 58 open findings, 51 of them blocking. All 58 compared revision-1 parses. They went from 10 to 29 to 51 blocking over three runs, only through the 3-run repeat rule. 398 revision-1 accessions were queued, at about 96 a run. Until the queue drains, the check keeps reporting a parser fault that is already fixed as a data disagreement, and the repeat rule promotes it. This is the "escalation counts evaluations, not evidence" shape from v1.1.317.

### Change

- `QC705_FACT_SOURCE_AGREEMENT` reads the newest instance read's revision: `meta_ingest_recipe.args_json.rev`, where a missing value means 1, as in `filingFactsRereadQueue`. If the revision is older than `FILING_FACTS_PARSER_REVISION`, the accession is not compared. It returns a non-firing evaluation **with no value**, carrying `evidence.awaitingReread`, `parserRevision` and `currentRevision`. The distribution therefore covers only current-revision comparisons, and `evaluated_count − distribution.n` is the number awaiting re-read.
- An open finding on such an accession closes as `rule_changed`, with a note naming the read and its revision.
- 03, QC-705 row: the rule, and why the finding closes rather than staying open at warning.

### Why close, not keep open at warning

The finding's only evidence is a comparison of a parse the current parser no longer makes. This check no longer makes that claim. That is a fact about the check, the same kind of fact as a retired key shape, so the gate can state it.

Kept open at warning, the finding would hold its domain-day at warning on a claim nobody can currently support. Its 3-run counter would also measure how long the re-read queue is, not how long the data has disagreed.

Closing loses nothing. The fingerprint is kept. If the re-read still disagrees, the finding reopens as a recurrence, at its base severity (warning) with its counter at 1. Any escalation is then earned again, on current-revision evidence.

The cost is that a filing the queue never reaches stays unjudged. The awaiting count makes that visible.
