# v1.1.248 — 2026-09-15

Three faults found while validating the 2026-09-15 collector run. The run itself
was healthy — 6,001 calls, 7 non-2xx, finished 18:58:52 ET, gate generated and
the email accepted by the relay. Everything below is about what the run did with
what it collected, and about the alert that was supposed to tell the operator.

## 1. The idempotence key had no date, so a stable body deduped across days

`captureResponse` short-circuits on `dedup_hash`, which was
`sha256(provider + endpoint + params_json + body)`. The premise written into the
dictionary — *"a fresh call returns a different body"* — is true of a quote and
false of a **list**. A ticker's expiration dates are the same bytes every day
until one rolls off.

So on the second daily run, 496 of 541 expiration responses hashed equal to the
previous day's, returned `deduplicated: true`, and wrote **no
`raw_option_expiration` row for the new date**. QC-401 then reported, correctly,
that 91.68 % of the chain subset "returned no expirations" — while every one of
those calls had been made, answered `200`, and paid for.

Measured in the collector window (18:03:37–18:58:52 ET):

| provider | calls | captured in `meta_response` |
|---|---|---|
| etrade | 4,867 | **4,365** |
| polygon | 1,090 | 1,090 |
| edgar | 31 | 31 |
| finnhub | 9 | 9 |
| treasury | 1 | 1 |

The entire 502-response gap is the expirations endpoint. Independently: comparing
E*Trade response sizes per symbol against the previous night gives **499
identical, 20 changed, 18 new** — against QC-401's 496.

It did not show on 2026-09-14 because that was the *first* daily run, with
nothing to dedupe against.

**Fixed:** `as_of_date` is in the key (`responseDedupHash`). Crash recovery and
replay still no-op within a date; a different date is never a repeat, however
identical the bytes. `study-pin.ts` carried its own copy of the formula and now
calls the shared one — an operator action repeated on a later date would have
collided on the UNIQUE index. `replay.ts` copies stored hashes and is unaffected.

Dictionary v15 → **v16** (description only; no DDL change).

## 2. The constructed OSI symbol padded with spaces; E*Trade pads with dashes

`ref_sticky_contract.osi_symbol` is matched by equality against
`raw_option_contract_snapshot.osi_symbol`. `osi.ts` built the root as
`padEnd(6, ' ')`, on a docstring claiming the format came from a live response.
It did not — the OCC specification pads with spaces, E*Trade sends dashes.

Measured against captured bytes, gen-002, 2026-09-15:

- `raw_option_contract_snapshot.osi_symbol` (E*Trade's `osiKey`, verbatim):
  `ACGL--260918C00095000`, `BMNR--270115P00095000`
- `ref_sticky_contract.osi_symbol` (built here): `AIZ   261016C00280000`

So the join could not match for **any** root shorter than six characters. Every
pin was unhonoured — QC-409 at 267 findings on 2026-09-14 and 415 on 2026-09-15,
growing by one per new recommendation and never resolving — and `sticky_reason`
was never stamped on a captured contract. This is precisely the failure the
file's own header warned about ("it would pin a contract that never matches
anything, which is the worst available failure").

Nothing threw, and no test caught it, because **every fixture spelt `osiKey` the
way the builder did**: a reader tested against a fixture written from the reader.

**Fixed:** `OSI_PAD = '-'`. `osi.test.ts` is now anchored on symbols copied out
of `raw_option_contract_snapshot` rather than typed by hand.
`repairStickyOsiPadding()` converts the pins already written — closing the old
range and opening a corrected one, keeping `source_response_id` and `origin_ref`,
never rewriting in place, because the old symbol is the evidence for the 415
findings it caused. Idempotent; runs at the head of the chains phase.

## 3. The gate email: "sent" was an assumption, and the body was a dump

Two RED gate emails (2026-09-14, 2026-09-15) were logged `sent` with no error and
did not arrive. Two separate problems:

- **The send path discarded the relay's verdict.** `nodemailer.sendMail` rejects
  only when *every* recipient is refused, so a partial rejection resolved and was
  logged as success; `accepted`, `rejected`, `response` and `messageId` were all
  thrown away. `EmailService.send` now returns them, and the collector hook logs
  them with the body size, flagging a body over Gmail's ~102 KB clip threshold.
  A partial rejection logs `collector-email:partial` at ERROR.
- **The body listed every finding, one per line.** 773 blocking rows, uncapped,
  plus repeats and no-longer-observed. Past the clip threshold and unreadable
  well before it: 415 identical QC-409 lines are one fact printed 415 times, and
  they buried the eight other checks that each needed a different decision. Each
  section now collapses to one line per check — count, age, up to three real rows,
  then `… and N more`. The total is never dropped. A test asserts the HTML stays
  under 102 KB when a single check fires 500 times.

**Not yet established:** why the two emails did not arrive. The relay accepted
them; the instrumentation above is what makes the next one diagnosable rather
than a guess. Check the spam folder for `[TA-Data]` in the meantime.
