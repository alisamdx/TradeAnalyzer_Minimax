TradeAnalyzer only. The BPS-19 harness, and a shared library under both spread study scripts.
(Written 2026-09-22 as v1.1.315 and left uncommitted; that number was used by later releases.)
**No application code changed, no rule changed.**

## BPS-19 — prefer the monthly inside 21–35 DTE, or keep nearest-30?

BPS-4 established that the whole 13.6% cost gap between a ~21 and a ~30 DTE spread is **expiry type,
not tenor** (with both controls on: 15 pairs, −0.9%). What nearest-30 is really doing is landing on a
third-Friday monthly about two times in three and inheriting its depth — open interest 460/565 against
a weekly's 37/42. That is a good reason to keep the rule and a bad reason to keep it *implicit*,
because where the nearest-30 expiry is a weekly the rule takes the weekly even with a monthly sitting
inside the same window.

**BPS-4's harness cannot answer this.** It compares DTE *buckets* (~21 vs ~30), which is not the rule.
`fr-spread-monthly-rule-study.mjs` runs the two **pickers** over the same captured expiry set:

- **A** — incumbent: in 21–35 DTE, before earnings, nearest 30, ties to the shorter.
- **B** — candidate: the monthly in that same eligible set if there is one, **else exactly A's pick**.

The fallback is load-bearing. B is a strict refinement, so the arms can only differ where a real
choice existed; without it the comparison would quietly include runs with no monthly available and
measure something else.

## Both halves of the wheel's rejection are measured, not assumed

The wheel's version of this rule was tested on realized P&L and **rejected** (2026-08-04, v1.1.126) for
two reasons, and the harness reports each:

1. **The saving was never paid.** Monthly-vs-weekly friction was 19.6% vs 40.3% of credit — and
   *conditional on clearing the entry gate* it was 6.9% vs 6.0%, indistinguishable. The whole gap sat
   in structures already refused. So the report gives a gate-clearance 2×2 and a cost comparison
   restricted to candidates **both** rules can trade. A saving only available on trades you would not
   take is not a saving.
2. **Snapping away from ~30 DTE landed on a contract thinner than the weekly it replaced**, because
   the monthly's depth advantage is a 23–33 DTE phenomenon. So the report gives both picks' open
   interest and counts how often B's is lower — the failure mode, measured in either direction.

It also reports the **dilution**: B equals A wherever no monthly exists, so the book-level effect is
the disagreements scaled by how rarely they happen, which is the honest size of the lever.

That was a one-leg CSP behind a `liquidityScore ≥ 8` gate; this is a four-fill spread behind a cost
gate refusing 47% of candidates. Genuinely a different question — and the same trap is available.

## A shared library, because this is the fourth copy

`scripts/lib/spread-study-lib.mjs` now holds the profile, the fill convention, the construction, the
monthly test, both pickers, the loader and the paired-difference reporter. `fr-spread-dte-cost-study`
was refactored onto it; **its 25 self-test assertions are the regression check for that refactor and
stayed green**.

The reason is on the record: `packages/core/scripts/exception.mjs` was written after three sibling
scripts had each grown their own copy of four safety checks, and it had none of them — *a copy in each
caller is not a safeguard, it is a list of the callers somebody remembered, and the next script is
written after the list.* Two harnesses reimplementing `buildBullPutSpread` is exactly that shape.

## Self-test — 19/19

Dates checked rather than assumed (2026-06-19 **is** a third Friday; 2026-06-12, 06-26 and 07-03 are
not; from 2026-05-29 they sit at 21, 28 and 35 DTE). Twelve runs where A takes the 28-DTE weekly and B
takes the 21-DTE monthly; three where the monthly *is* the nearest-30 pick, so the arms must agree;
two with no monthly at all, testing the fallback; and one where an earnings date excludes the two
later expiries from **both** arms, which must be reported as binding on exactly one run.

The disagreeing fixture makes B **cheaper and thinner at the same time**, so the cost report and the
open-interest report must move independently — that combination is precisely what killed the wheel's
version. And an inverted fixture flips the quotes: the median must go positive, the gate 2×2 must
reverse, and B must stop being the thinner pick. A harness that can only report one direction is
decoration.
