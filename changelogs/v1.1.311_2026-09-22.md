# v1.1.311 — 2026-09-22

TradeAnalyzer only. A study harness for BPS-4. **No application code changed, no rule changed.**

## BPS-4 said the evidence disagreed with itself. It doesn't — one side describes a discarded rule.

The list recorded a standing contradiction: per-trade, **21 DTE beat 30 and 35 at every delta**; in
the portfolio simulation, **30 DTE returned +75.1% against 21 DTE's +61.3%**. Reading the harness
resolves it.

**The DTE sweep runs on the draft exits.** `fr-bull-put-spread-study.mjs:653` builds its grid from
`BASE` — `pt 0.5, sl 2, ts 15`. BPS-13 found the stop plus time stop **halved** the portfolio return,
and both were dropped. A fixed 15-day time stop is not DTE-neutral: it cuts at **71%** of a 21-DTE
trade's life and **43%** of a 35-DTE one. The study's own table says exactly that (Δ0.30):

| DTE | target | stop | **time stop** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 21 | 71% | 11% | **18%** |
| 30 | 58% | 6% | **36%** |
| 35 | 53% | 5% | **42%** |

The longer-dated arms are force-closed mid-flight at more than twice the rate, forfeiting the theta
they exist to collect. §4 is not measuring DTE — it is measuring how much of each trade's life the
time stop lets you keep.

**The confound is larger than the effect.** At Δ0.25, w5%: 21 DTE draft exits mean **2.57%**, 30 DTE
draft exits **1.72%** — the 0.85-point gap that made the case. Removing the time stop takes 30 DTE to
**3.80%** (§4b), a **2.08-point** move: **2.4× the finding**. Under the adopted exits 30 DTE's mean
already beats 21 DTE's draft-exit mean.

**And the comparison was never re-run.** §4b and §4c switch to `LEAN` (50% target, no stop, no time
stop) and hold **DTE fixed at 30**, sweeping wing × delta instead. There is no per-trade 21-vs-30
comparison under the shipped exit rule anywhere in #43.

**The portfolio side is one pair, and the list cites the wrong run.** `portfolioConfigs` contains
exactly one 21-DTE row and **no 35-DTE row at all**. `+75.1 / +61.3` is `bull-put-spread-v4-flat.md`,
the **flat $0.03/leg** run:

| | 30 DTE | 21 DTE | gap |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| flat costs | 75.1% | 61.3% | **13.8 pts** |
| quoted costs | 51.8% | 47.7% | **4.1 pts** |

Realistic costs remove 70% of the advantage — the 30-DTE arm loses 23 points to them, the 21-DTE arm
14. On the more realistic run the case for 30 is 4 points on a single simulated path where the file's
own footer warns nine configurations were compared. 21 DTE also carried the lower drawdown (9.6% vs
10.2%) on 337 trades against 263.

## What can settle it, and what cannot

**Shadow mode cannot.** It targets 30, so stored DTEs run 24–32 and cluster at 32. Unlike BPS-3 and
BPS-10, this question generates no observations by construction — the rule under test is the rule
producing the data.

**The data is already paid for.** `SPREAD_CAPTURE` fetches every expiry in a **14–45 day** window for
each entry ticker, every run, at real bid/ask — wider than the mode's own 21–35 window. Seven capture
days are stored.

That matters because the study's weakest assumption is the one a DTE comparison leans on hardest: the
long wing is Black-Scholes plus a flat "vol points per 10% OTM" skew, and §3 moves the 30-DTE answer
from **0.277 to 0.081 %/day** across skew 0→6. Real skew steepens as expiry approaches, so the
modelling error is itself DTE-dependent — the wrong instrument for this question.

## `scripts/fr-spread-dte-cost-study.mjs`

Builds the mode's spread at 21 and at 30 DTE from the **same stored chain**, paired on
ticker+date+slot, and reports credit, round-trip cost, cost share, and who clears BPS-9's 25% gate.
Deliberately silent on returns — nothing is settled yet, and a cost measurement is what BPS-3
established actually discriminates between these trades.

Three things it gets right on purpose:

- **Paired only.** A ticker-run counts only if a spread builds in *both* buckets. A naive pooled
  weekly-vs-monthly friction comparison **inverts** in this codebase (2026-08-04, the expiry-selection
  finding): the names landing in each bucket differ. Hold the name, the moment and the quote fixed;
  vary only the expiry. Two buckets resolving to the same contract are refused, because comparing a
  row against itself reports a perfect tie that reads as a clean null.
- **The 25% cost gate is an outcome, never a filter.** `buildBullPutSpread` refuses above it, so
  reusing it would drop the expensive half of the distribution under study — the censoring BPS-3 had
  to parse back out of rejection sentences.
- **The monthly share of each bucket is reported**, because it could be the whole answer: a ~30 DTE
  bucket lands on a third-Friday monthly far more often than a ~21 one, and the monthly's open
  interest advantage in this codebase is a 23–33 DTE phenomenon. A same-expiry-type subgroup is shown
  when there are enough pairs, and named as too few when there are not.

`reason = 'entry'` rows only — those come from the uniform 14–45 sweep, while a `track` row exists
only because that expiry was already picked, which is a selection effect on the long bucket.

## Self-test — 15/15, `scripts/selftest-spread-dte-cost.mjs`

The harness reimplements `buildBullPutSpread` and `fillPrice`, because a `scripts/*.mjs` harness
cannot import the real service. Fixtures with one unambiguous right answer each: a wide 21-DTE chain
that must fail the gate at 21 and clear it at 30; a tight pair that must clear both; a chain with no
strike in the wing band, which must be dropped from the pairs and counted under "could not build"; a
ticker with only one expiry; and one expiration stored under two DTE values, which must be refused as
a self-comparison.

**And an inverted fixture**, because a check that can only report "the shorter expiry costs more" is
decoration — the shape of the conviction band that could never fire and the liquidity gate that always
did. With the quotes reversed the median difference must go negative and both names must land in the
"tradable only at 21" cell.
