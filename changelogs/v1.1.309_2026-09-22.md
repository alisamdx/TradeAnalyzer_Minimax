# v1.1.309 — 2026-09-22

## QC-702 was measuring a stock split

### The finding

> IESC: EDGAR 19,924,356 vs reference 39,848,712 shares (50.0%)

The ratio is exactly 2.0000, which is the tell. Both numbers are correct:

| side | value | observed |
|---|---|---|
| EDGAR `dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding` | 19,924,356 | stated **2026-07-27** (Q3 10-Q cover page) |
| Polygon `raw_ticker_reference.shares_outstanding` | 39,848,712 | observed **2026-09-11** |

And `raw_split` holds the answer:

```
IESC  2026-08-24  1:2
```

IES Holdings split two-for-one on 2026-08-24, between the two observations. The
EDGAR figure is pre-split, the reference figure post-split, and the check was
comparing them as if they were on one scale. It was measuring the corporate
action, not the data.

### The fix

QC-702 now carries the EDGAR figure across any split strictly after the fact's
`period_end` and on or before the reference's `as_of_date`, and compares on one
scale. Measured on the 2026-09-22 generation: **75 evaluated, 0 fired**, IESC
reading

> IESC: EDGAR 39,848,712 vs reference 39,848,712 shares (0.0%) [EDGAR carried ×2 across 1 split(s) on 2026-08-24]

The window boundaries are the two observation dates and nothing else. A split on
or before `period_end` is already inside the EDGAR figure; one after the
reference observation is inside neither. Applying either would manufacture the
disagreement the adjustment exists to remove.

**The adjustment is named in the finding, never applied silently**, and the
evidence keeps both `edgarSharesAsFiled` and the carried `edgarShares` — a
figure that has been moved must stay checkable against the filing it came from.
That is the same discipline as the backtest scale guard, which prints excluded
rows rather than dropping them.

### The failure direction

An incomplete `raw_split` makes this **under**-adjust and therefore **fire**.
That is the right way round: a missing corporate action shows up as a loud
disagreement rather than as a silent correction, so the check cannot quietly
explain away a real fault with an adjustment it invented. A test pins it.

### The broader shape

This is the same family as the split-repair work and the stored option legs:
**two quantities captured at different times are not comparable across a
corporate action, and nothing about either number says so.** The existing app
has been bitten by it three times now — unadjusted splits in
`historical_prices`, strikes frozen in `daily_briefs.picks` sitting on a
different scale from a repaired series, and now a share count.

Every one of them looked like a data error and was a timing difference.

### Noted in passing, not fixed

`raw_ticker_reference` for IESC holds exactly one row, from **run 1 on
2026-09-11** — the universe seed. The reference side of this comparison can
therefore be weeks stale, and a split after the last reference refresh would
break the comparison in the other direction. The adjustment reads the
reference's own `as_of_date`, so it stays correct either way, but the staleness
itself is worth a look separately.
