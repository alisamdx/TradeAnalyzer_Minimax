# v1.1.259 — 2026-09-17

## `qc204-triage.mjs` — the evidence for 151 rulings, on one screen

```bash
node packages/core/scripts/qc204-triage.mjs --latest [--all] [--csv out.csv]
```

Read-only, against a **snapshot** (it refuses anything under `AppData` or named
`data.sqlite`). For every open QC-204 discontinuity it prints the step, the nearest
clean split ratio and its error, the step day's own intraday range, median volume for
the 20 bars either side and their ratio, the dormancy gap, and whether QC-205 or QC-209
flagged **that same step**.

The `suggests` column is a sort order for a human, never a verdict. The three causes of a
price step need three different remedies and only one is repairable, so the point is to
group 151 findings into a handful of rulings rather than to decide them.

On the 2026-09-17 snapshot, 151 findings across 63 tickers:

```
move?                    95     illiquid-shell           22
dormant-resume (QC-209)  13     reuse (QC-205)           11
reuse?                    4     split?                    3
unclear                   2     dormant-resume?           1
```

## Two things the triage found that the counts alone did not

**1. A high volume ratio is NOT sufficient for ticker reuse — a transformative corporate
event clears the same bar.** The documented rule is that reuse shifts the volume regime
≥ 15x permanently while genuine moves stay ≤ 5x (BNY 76x, SPCX 53,592x against MRNA's
5.0x). Measured here, three steps clear 15x on names that were **already liquid and
trading continuously**:

```
GME  2024-05-13   +74.4%    4.9M → 74.7M   15.3x   20 of 20 prior sessions traded
SMMT 2022-12-06  +194.3%    156k →  8.1M   52.1x   20 of 20
BMNR 2025-06-30  +694.8%     50k → 28.4M  571.1x   16 of 20
```

That matters because QC-205's remedy is **destructive** — truncate `historical_prices`
and `iv_history` before the splice. A reused ticker is a *dormant shell* whose symbol was
reassigned; a ticker printing 4.9M shares a day cannot be one, whatever its volume ratio
does next. **The prior liquidity LEVEL is the missing half of the discriminator, and it
is free**: it is already in the same window the ratio is computed from. Left as an
observation, not a check change — QC-205's threshold is `measured`, and these are three
cases, not a study.

**2. Keying a per-step finding by ticker multiplies it.** The first version of this
script marked a ticker's every step with QC-205's flag, so BMNR's single 2025 splice
tagged all 26 of its steps as reuse — while their own volume ratios run 0.27–3.94. It now
matches on `ticker date`. Same shape as the SOLS windows that overlapped: a finding about
one event, spread across a ticker's whole history, reads as a much bigger problem than it
is.

## What the 151 actually are

- **~97 genuine moves** (95 `move?` + both `unclear`, which are CNC's July 2025 guidance
  withdrawal and RBLX's Q3 2021 print — each just outside a threshold). These want an
  acceptance with the 30-day TTL, because providers backfill corporate actions late.
- **22 illiquid-shell** — almost all BMNR before its 2025 pivot, median volume **31
  shares a day**. A price step in a security that barely trades is not data about a
  company. One ruling covers the class.
- **3 `split?`**, and these are the only ones where a re-fetch is the right remedy. `DOC
  2024-03-04` is the clearest: ratio exactly **1.500**, step-day range **2.3 %**, volume
  1.84x — open/high/low/close all already in the new scale, which is the unadjusted-split
  signature. `SOLS 2023-07-26` is the same shape (ratio 1.500, range 0.0 %).
- **13 + 11** carry QC-209 and QC-205 findings on the same step and should be ruled
  through those checks, with the liquidity caveat above.

## Not done, deliberately

No rulings were made. `exception.mjs` writes them and opens the live generation, which
needs a non-containerised shell with both apps closed — and an acceptance carries a
30-day expiry, so a wrong one comes back.
