## QC-603 no longer fires on a new listing whose first report is not yet due

**What was firing.** Of QC-603's 17 findings on 2026-09-18, seven looked like new listings. Their `list_date`s split them into two groups:

| ticker | listed | days | first report due? |
|---|---|---|---|
| HONA | 2026-06-15 | 95 | no |
| SPCX | 2026-06-12 | 98 | no |
| CBRS | 2026-05-14 | 127 | not quite |
| JAN | 2026-03-20 | 182 | yes |
| SUNB | 2026-03-02 | 200 | yes |
| MDLN | 2025-12-17 | 275 | yes |
| BMNR | 1997-12-17 | — | yes |

For the first three there is no date to have missed: a company that has never reported and has not announced its first report is doing what a new listing does. The others have been public long enough to have reported at least once, so "never dated" there is the same Finnhub coverage gap as SWKS or IOT — v1.1.270's E\*Trade source is the fix for those, not an exemption.

**The rule.** A ticker never dated by any source, whose latest `list_date` is within **135 days**, is evaluated at 0 and does not fire. 135 = its first full quarter (at most 90 days) + the 45-day 10-Q deadline of a non-accelerated filer, which every newly public company is. The bound is the company's own filing obligation, not a tuned number, so a listing that should have reported and still has no date fires again. The evidence records `listDate` and `newlyListed`. The grace covers **never dated** only: a new listing that was dated once and then dropped out of the calendar still fires. A ticker with no `list_date` gets no grace.

**On 2026-09-18's data:** QC-603 fires on 14 instead of 17 — CBRS, HONA and SPCX excused; CBRS's grace ends 2026-09-26.
