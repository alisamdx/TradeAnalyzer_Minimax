# v1.1.171 — 2026-09-03

## T1 — the short put's 0.30 delta target is validated. No change.

`strategy-lab-service.ts` builds every `short-naked-put` at a hardcoded `deltaTarget: 0.30` — one
constant for every ticker in every IV regime, borrowed from tastytrade convention and never tested
here. It was the least-audited number in the wheel path (`docs/wheel-parameters.md`). It now has
evidence, and the evidence supports it.

### The method the inventory proposed was impossible

`docs/wheel-parameters.md` said to "re-derive the CSP at 0.20 / 0.25 / 0.30 / 0.35 from the stored
chains." **There are no stored chains** — `options_cache` is a live cache holding 0 rows, and
`daily_briefs.picks` records only the contract chosen. Synthesising other strikes through
Black-Scholes on the stored IV would ignore the volatility smile, which understates OTM put premium
and so penalises low-delta arms — a directional bias on the exact axis under test. That was not
done.

What exists instead is better: `options_premium_history` holds one **real** CSP per ticker per day
per strategy with its real `delta`, `premium` and `strike`, settled against the real close at
expiry. Wheel's band spans 0.20–0.30 and income's reaches 0.35, so the stored history is itself an
observational experiment across the range that matters. `scripts/fr-wheel-delta-study.mjs`.

**16,902 settled wheel CSPs · 1,005 tickers · 31 dates (2026-06-15 → 2026-07-29).**

### A — all settled wheel CSPs, binned by delta

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20–0.23 | 2,156 | 12.2% | +1.03% | 54 | 35 |

### B — matched pairs, which is the real test

Wheel and income sometimes select **different strikes on the same ticker, same day, same expiry**.
Ticker, regime, IV and DTE are then held constant by construction and only delta differs.
**1,151 pairs → 937 independent episodes** (collapsed by ticker + expiry).

| | low delta | high delta |
|---|---|---|
| mean \|delta\| | 0.23 | 0.28 |
| premium / strike | 1.93% | 2.47% |
| assigned | 15.2% | 17.6% |
| mean return on capital | +0.48% | **+1.14%** |

**Paired difference +0.65 pts per episode; higher delta won 562 of 718 decided episodes (78.3%).**
The decomposition: +0.54 pts of extra premium against +2.4 pts of extra assignment — the premium
more than covers it. And the **dose–response is monotone**, which is what separates a real effect
from noise:

| delta gap | n | mean high − low |
|---|---|---|
| 0.02–0.04 | 608 | +0.22 pts |
| 0.04–0.07 | 369 | +0.41 pts |
| 0.07+ | 174 | **+2.26 pts** |

| 0.20–0.24 | 6,743 | 14.3% | +1.11% | 57 |

Monotone to 0.35 — and the top two bins carry *lower* IV rank (47–48 vs 57–59), so the confound
runs against them. That strengthens the reading rather than weakening it.

### D — why this still does not mean "raise it"

Sections A–C measure the **premium seller's** outcome: dollars on collateral, settled at expiry. A
wheel is not purely a premium business — assignment converts to stock, and v1.1.169 measured that
where the cost basis lands matters (basis at or below the 200-day average separated outcomes by
17.4pp per episode, excess of SPY). Delta moves the two in **opposite** directions.

| delta | mean return | basis ≤ SMA200 | basis vs SMA200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20–0.23 | +1.03% | **70.0%** | −8.2% |
| 0.23–0.26 | +1.06% | 63.2% | −3.6% |
| 0.26–0.28 | +1.26% | 64.9% | −5.8% |
| 0.28–0.30 | +1.20% | **62.9%** | −6.3% |

Moving from ~0.21 to ~0.29 buys ~0.17pp of return on collateral and costs ~7pp of picks landing at
or below the long-run average. **Those two numbers are not nettable** — one is return on collateral
at expiry, the other is excess-of-SPY over the 30 days *after* expiry measured from the cost basis.
They are different quantities over different windows, and combining them would manufacture a
precision neither has.

### Verdict — keep 0.30

- **Nothing supports lowering it.** Every section says lower delta returns less, and section B says
  so under matched conditions with a monotone dose–response.
- **Raising it is not supported strongly enough.** Section C is monotone to 0.35, but its top bin
  is 542 rows from one regime, section D shows cost-basis quality eroding as delta rises, and the
  own-at-strike finding it trades against is itself thin (12 episodes). Under FR-SEY-5 that is not
  a case for moving a live parameter.
- 0.30 sits at the top of the wheel band, where the premium evidence is strongest and the
  cost-basis cost has not yet compounded. **It passes where it stands.**

### Caveats

- **Range**: real quotes span |delta| 0.20–0.35 only. This says nothing about 0.10 or 0.45.
- **One regime**: 31 entry dates in a 6.5-week window — the same caution that killed the SPY-regime
  gate and the overbought guard.
- **Section B's pairs are a non-random subset**: a pair exists only where the two code paths
  disagreed about the strike, i.e. names where the chain offered a meaningfully different one.
- **Held to expiry at intrinsic** — no early management, roll, or covered call after assignment. A
  wheel operator does all three and all three cushion assignment, so the true cost of a higher
  delta is *smaller* than sections A–C show. This biases toward raising delta, and is another
  reason not to act on a one-regime sample.
