# v1.1.307 — 2026-09-22

## QC-601 manufactured its own worst finding

### What happened

The 2026-09-22 gate reported:

> FERG (finnhub) nearest report moved 2026-09-22 -> 2026-12-07, 76 days later

Seventy-six days is the bulk-cap truncation signature — the fault QC-601 exists
for, the one that stored NVDA's August report as November in the existing app.
It was also, measured across the whole generation, **the single largest later
slip in 121 of them.**

It was not a slip. FERG's stored rows:

| run | as_of | report_date | fiscal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–27 | 2026-09-14 … 09-21 | **2026-09-22** | FY2026 Q4 |
| 8–27 | 2026-09-14 … 09-21 | **2026-12-07** | FY2026 Q3 |
| 28 | 2026-09-22 | 2026-12-07 | FY2026 Q3 |

Both dates were stored from run 8. On 2026-09-22 — the day FERG reported —
Finnhub dropped the Q4 row and the nearest future date became the Q3 date that
had been sitting beside it for seven runs. Nothing was truncated. Nothing was
lost. The calendar rolled.

### The bug

The excusal was strict:

```ts
const rolledAfterReport = r.prev_date < r.new_as_of;
```

`'2026-09-22' < '2026-09-22'` is `false`, so a nearest date landing **exactly
on the run's own date** was not excused — and a ticker reporting *today* is
precisely the case where the roll is most certainly legitimate. One character.

Now `<=`, in a named function both call sites share. The comparison existed in
**two copies** — the live reading and `rejudgeSlipFindings` — which is the
patch-one-call-site shape this project keeps meeting; extracting it is most of
the fix.

Nothing is given up by including the boundary. The collector runs after the
close, so a report dated today has happened (BMO hours ago, AMC within the
hour), and a truncation that dropped a date equal to today would roll to the
next quarter anyway, because that date has passed.

### Why it is the same bug as v1.1.304

QC-602 reported ACN as 80 days apart when the real disagreement was four.
QC-601 reported FERG as 76 days later when the real move was none. Both were
**true-looking findings carrying fictional numbers**, and in both cases the
fiction was the signature of a *more serious* fault than the one actually
present — so the number spent the operator's attention in the wrong place.
A finding that is wrong about its magnitude is worse than a false positive.

### The distribution, and what was deliberately NOT changed

Measured over the generation: 121 later slips after excusals.

| slip | count |
|---|---|
| 1 day | 53 |
| 2–3 | 11 |
| 4–7 | 41 |
| 8–14 | 12 |
| **15–22** | **3** |
| 23–45 | 0 |
| **46+** | **1 (FERG, this bug)** |

Median 3 days, p90 8. Four of 121 exceed the 14-day threshold: MKTX 15, OWL 17,
M 22 — and FERG's 76.

All three survivors are Finnhub refining a date it had estimated, not
truncation: Macy's moving to 2026-12-03 (its Q3 is historically early December),
Blue Owl to 2026-10-29, MarketAxess **reverting** to the 2026-11-05 it had held
for five runs after a two-run excursion to 2026-10-21. That last one is worth
noting on its own — the check does not measure nearer moves, so a spurious
nearer move is invisible going in and fires on the way back out.

The empty 23–45 band is suggestive that 14 is drawn a little tight. **The
threshold was not moved, and the reason matters more than the answer: with
FERG reclassified, this dataset contains zero confirmed truncation events.**
Raising a threshold when every sample is a negative is how a check becomes
unable to fire — the conviction band that could never fire, in a new place.
Revisit when a real truncation has been observed, or on a longer sample.
