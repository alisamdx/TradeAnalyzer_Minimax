# v1.1.133 — 2026-08-07

## Realized-outcome A/B: enabling `WHEEL_GRADED_LIQUIDITY` is REJECTED

The evidence v1.1.131 was waiting on. No behaviour change — the flag was already off and now stays
off on data rather than on caution.

### Method

`scripts/fr-income-liquidity-realized-ab.mjs`, run on **income** rows because they are the only
ones that kept the liquidity inputs: all 22,302 income rows carry bid/ask/open_interest, all 21,127
wheel rows are NULL (v1.1.132). Re-scoring a historical recommendation needs the inputs, not the
verdict.

The income composite is fully reconstructible point-in-time:

| signal | weight | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ivRank` | 30 | stored `iv_rank` |
| `premiumRiskRatio` | 22 | stored `annualized_return` |
| `recentVolatility` | 20 | 20-day realized vol from `historical_prices`, as of `as_of_date` |
| `trendNeutral` | 15 | permanently null while ADX is out of scoring — as in live |
| `optionLiquidity` | 13 | recomputed from stored bid/ask/OI under **both** schemes |

Outcome settles at intrinsic, no path or IV assumptions:
`pnl/share = premium − max(0, strike − close@expiry)`, expressed as % of strike (collateral).

**6,777 settled CSPs · 937 tickers · 16 dates (2026-06-15 → 2026-07-08).** Universe baseline:
19.9% assigned, +0.61% mean return on capital.

### Result — worse, not neutral

Top-5 per date by full composite:

| arm | assigned | mean return on capital | mean liquidity score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| legacy | 60.0% | **−2.92%** | 4.3 |
| graded | 71.3% | **−4.80%** | 5.2 |

**Δ −1.88 pts per trade.** Pick sets differ on 15/16 dates. Holds at top-10: **Δ −1.33**.

### The deeper finding — liquidity does not predict outcomes at all

Spearman(score, realized return) across all 6,777 rows:

```
legacy  -0.015          graded  +0.032
```

Neither is a relationship. And by quartile the graded score is mildly *anti*-predictive:

| graded band | assigned | mean return | ivRank | realized vol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bottom 25% | 14.8% | **+0.95%** | 49 | **43%** |
| mid 50% | 19.2% | +0.65% | 49 | 46% |
| top 25% | 26.6% | **+0.21%** | 51 | **55%** |

The last column is the explanation. IV rank is flat across the bands (~50), but **realized
volatility rises from 43% to 55%**. Liquidity tracks attention; attention tracks names that are
moving; names that are moving breach short puts. The adverse relation is selection, not causation —
and it means weighting liquidity *positively* in a short-premium ranking composite tilts the book
toward exactly the wrong names.

### What this overturns, and what it doesn't

v1.1.131's calibration was **correct on its own terms.** The graded formula really is a far better
predictor of execution cost (Spearman −0.419 → −0.799 against real fill cost). Both statements hold
at once:

- it is a **better cost model**, and
- a **worse ranking signal**,

because **liquidity is an execution-cost property, not an outcome-prediction property**. It answers
*can I trade this?*, not *should I?*.

The v2 engine already uses it the right way — as a hard gate (`liquidityScore ≥ 8` in
`EntryPolicy`). The category error is carrying it as **13% (income) / 9% (wheel) of a ranking
composite**. The graded formula and its flag are therefore **kept**: a better-calibrated score makes
a better gate, which is the role that survives.

**Follow-up recorded, not taken:** the indicated fix is to reduce or remove `optionLiquidity`'s
composite weight, not to calibrate it further. That is its own FR-SEY-5-gated study.

**`WHEEL_MONTHLY_FIRST_EXPIRY` is untouched by this.** It changes the contract actually
recommended, so its benefit is a real fill-cost saving on the trade you would place — a different
mechanism from ranking, which this study does not measure.

### Caveats

- **One regime.** 16 entry dates inside a single three-week window. The sibling FR-SEY studies used
  1,000+ picks over ~1.1 years. Section A has 6,777 observations and is far better powered than
  section B's ~80 trades per arm.
- **Income is a proxy for wheel** — same signal, same `findSuitableChain`, but delta band 0.20–0.35
  vs 0.20–0.30 and weight 13 vs 9.
- **Volume floored to 0 in both arms** — no historical row has it (migration 066 starts capturing
  it). Fair between arms, but it caps the graded scheme, whose point is grading components the
  legacy gates fail to register.
- **The absolute section-B numbers are pessimistic and are not what the live brief produces.** The
  reconstruction ranks on composite alone; live income also applies the Stage-3 earnings penalty
  (−20 composite — it strips exactly the earnings runners that blow up a short put), the liquidity
  hard gate, and the critic. The **delta between arms** is unaffected: both omit the same things.
  Read section B as *which scheme ranks better*, never as *income's P&L*.
- Held to expiry: no early management, no roll, no assignment-then-covered-call leg.
