# v1.1.310 — 2026-09-22

TradeAnalyzer only. BPS-10 instrumentation, plus the first read of the BPS-3 check shipped in v1.1.303.
**No selection rule changed.**

## BPS-3 read first, because it moves the argument

The v1.1.303 check has its first real sample — 52 paired rows, from the three arms' Monday and Tuesday
runs:

| Lab score | n | median cost share | over the 25% gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 | 16 | **44%** | 11 / 16 |
| 7–8 | 15 | **41%** | 11 / 15 |
| 9–10 | 10 | **20%** | 3 / 10 |
| 11–15 | 11 | **16.5%** | 2 / 11 |

Spearman **−0.51**. **The prediction was flat and it is not flat.** The argument for flat was structural:
`STRATEGY_RELEVANT_SLUGS.wheel` scores a one-leg structure, so the number cannot see the long wing — the
leg most likely to be illiquid — and should therefore say nothing about the cost of trading both. It
tracks anyway. The one-leg score is evidently a proxy for the whole chain's liquidity, wing included.
That is worth recording as an error of the same kind as v1.1.176's: **a mechanism read off a structure is
a hypothesis, and this project's data can usually test it.**

**Where the break is matters as much as that there is one.** `0–6` and `7–8` are one population (44 vs
41; 69% vs 73% over the gate) and `9–10` and `11–15` are another. So the bar that BPS-3 was originally
written around — raise 5 to 8 — would have landed in the middle of a flat stretch and separated nothing.
The evidenced cut is **between 8 and 9**. The band edges were fixed in v1.1.303, before any data, so the
break is not a chosen one.

**Nothing was raised, and the reason is not caution.** The 25% cost gate already measures this quantity
*directly*, on the two contracts actually traded, and it is the best-evidenced rule in #43 (the gate makes
the portfolio result insensitive to how costs are modelled). A liquidity bar at 9 would gate one quantity
twice — once directly, once by a proxy that carries a known risk tilt, since the liquid subset
underperforms on two independent samples. The one honest argument for raising it is **call budget**: a
liquidity refusal is free, a cost refusal costs a chain fetch. That is a real saving and not a reason to
move a gate on 52 rows.

Still censored, as the report says every run: the `0–6` band is mostly 5s and 6s. Nothing below the bar
is observable, so this says the score discriminates *where it operates* — never what a lower bar admits.

## BPS-10 — the eligible set, which is what this release adds

The ranking (08 §3.6, net credit ÷ max loss, top 5) is unvalidated and was nearly inert: "ranked below the
top 5" fired once in ten runs, because the pool was starved. With v1.1.292's leg rules and v1.1.300's
budget change it should now be deciding. Before studying the rule, count how often it faces a choice —
the wheel tiebreak was investigated at length (v1.1.174) before anyone noticed it faced one on 7 of 62
days, and the upstream gates were doing all the selecting.

- **`runs[].eligible`** — picks plus the candidates the cut dropped. Counted in SQL from the one reason
  `spread-mode.ts` writes at the cut, **not** derived from the rejection tally, which is grouped by kind
  across every run and cannot be split back per run. Every other rejection happened before the ranking, so
  `rejected` is the wrong denominator: premium's 10 rejections per run are mostly gate refusals, and the
  cut is choosing between six names, not fifteen.
- **`eligibility`** per pool — binding runs, names dropped, median and max eligible, and the size
  histogram. **`eligible == topN` is not binding**: every candidate was picked and the cut decided
  nothing, however full the pool looks. Counting that as a decision would make this the liquidity gate
  that always fired.
- Per pool, because the arms are nothing like each other. Averaged together they describe neither.

## Per-pool rejection counts (BPS-1)

`rejectionReasons[].pools`. `income` has now run three times and picked **nothing** — 0 picks against 7
rejections each run, with `overlap: 0` between all three arms. Summed across pools that is invisible: one
row saying "critic flag, n=17" hides an arm contributing no evidence at all. BPS-1 asked whether income
selects differently; it does, and the interesting question is now whether it selects *anything*.

never ran is distinguishable from it.
