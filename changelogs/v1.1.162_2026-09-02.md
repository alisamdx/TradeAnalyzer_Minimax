# v1.1.162 — 2026-09-02

## feat: Roll the Dice — one wheel entry from the brief, or an explicit nothing

The Daily Brief presents five strategies and ranks candidates within each. The operator's read,
after this session's P&L work, is that the wheel is the one that suits short cycles — and what
they wanted was a single trade they could place without reading the rest:

> *"a new function, I call it Roll the Dice … just 1 single opportunity for wheel strategy that
> will be the top most recommendation with no flag or caution … price is $300 or less … something
> absolutely I can close my eyes and do it, I know it comes with risk hence the name."*

### The design point: it must be able to return nothing

A "close your eyes" feature that always produces a pick would have to lower its bar on a bad day,
which is exactly when the bar matters. **"No dice today" is a first-class result**, carrying the
number of picks considered and the most common reason none survived. Every rejection is listed
with its ticker and gate, so the bar is *visible* rather than claimed.

### The gates

Hard, not weighted — the brief has already scored and ranked; this layer only removes.

| Gate | Rationale |
|---|---|
| critic verdict `pass` | a *caution* is something to read, and the premise is not reading |
| critic **not `degraded`** | a `pass` from the rule-based fallback is un-vetted, not an all-clear |
| price ≤ $300 (configurable) | a $300 stock is a $30,000 cash-secured commitment |
| constructible `short-naked-put` | `covered-call` is the other wheel slug but needs shares already |
| liquidity ≥ 8/15 | same bar the critic and the v2 entry gate use — you must be able to exit |
| **no earnings before expiry** | see below |
| net credit > 0 | a zero credit is a broken chain, not a trade |

Winner is the highest `compositeScore` among survivors. The card shows strike, expiry, credit,
capital required, **cost basis if assigned** and its discount to spot, annualized return, PoP,
grade, liquidity, and the critic's own note — plus the runner-up.

### Two gates that exist because of bugs found earlier in this same session

**Earnings is an exclusion, not the brief's −20 penalty.** A penalty on a high scorer still leaves
it on top; this session opened with *"the last few days brief recommended NVDA, when earnings was
days away."* For a pick meant to be acted on unread, a report inside the hold window disqualifies.

**An unknown or PAST stored earnings date fails closed.** `earnings_calendar` is keyed by ticker
with one row each and no history, so a `report_date` in the past means the row was never refreshed
after that report — the *next* date is unknown. Measured while building this: **57 of 1,069 rows**
were stale this way. My first implementation read a past date as "already reported, clear"; that
is the same mistake as the bug that opened the session — *"there is a date stored" is not "the
stored date is right."*

### Validation, and the part of it that is impossible

Retrospective over **62 stored wheel briefs** (2026-06-04 … 09-01), 248 picks: a pick on
**23 of 62 days (37%)**. Rejections: critic verdict 172, price ceiling 27, liquidity 24; 25 passed.

**The earnings gate cannot be validated retrospectively, and the replay proves why it's needed.**
Because the calendar has no history, a replay sees today's next-report date rather than the one
live on the brief date — so it records **zero** earnings rejections and still selects **NVDA on
2026-08-26, its actual earnings day**. That is precisely the failure this gate prevents, and it is
invisible to the only test available. The gate is correct by construction on live data; the replay
simply cannot see it.

### Scope

Pure filter over the stored brief: no scoring change, no provider call, nothing written. It does
not touch what the engine recommends, so FR-SEY-5 does not gate it — but the hit-rate measurement
above is recorded anyway, since the gate set is a selection policy.

`roll-the-dice.ts` + `scoring:roll-the-dice` IPC + a Daily Brief button and result card, styled
from theme variables (the v1.1.157 → v1.1.158 lesson: no second palette maintained by hand).
14 unit tests, each proving a gate REJECTS rather than that a happy path passes. Suite: 1058.
