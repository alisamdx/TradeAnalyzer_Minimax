# v1.1.170 — 2026-09-03

## `optionLiquidity` re-tested on 2.7× the sample — the disable is now data-backed, not judgement

No behaviour change. `optionLiquidity` was already disabled on wheel and income; this replaces the
reasoning under it.

### Why it was re-run

The operator asked to turn the signal back on, not remembering why it was off. It was off because
of v1.1.133/135 — but v1.1.135 was explicit that it had been applied **"on judgment, not on data"**,
because its weight sweep came back *non-monotone* (the middle worse than both endpoints) on ~80
trades per arm from a single three-week window. That is the correct reading of noise, and it left
the decision unsettled. Two more months of trades have since matured.

### The sample

`scripts/fr-income-liquidity-realized-ab.mjs`, unchanged — it selects everything settled up to
`MAX(historical_prices.date)`, so it extends itself.

| settled CSPs | 6,777 | **18,219** |

### Result — the sweep is now monotone

Top-5 per date, legacy formula, weight 0 = signal disabled entirely (leaves numerator *and*
denominator; the rest renormalise).

| weight | assigned | mean return on capital | total pts | picks changed vs w=13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **13** (then-current) | 54.2% | −0.47% | −72.5 | (baseline) |
| 9 | 58.7% | −0.27% | −42.4 | 21/31 dates |
| 6 | 63.9% | −0.20% | −30.6 | 30/31 dates |
| 3 | 61.9% | −0.08% | −12.9 | 31/31 dates |
| **0** (disabled) | 60.6% | **+0.54%** | **+83.7** | 31/31 dates |

Every reduction in weight improves the outcome, and disabling flips the arm from losing to
profitable — a 156-point swing across 155 trades, ≈1 pt per trade. **This is the exact test
v1.1.135 named**: "a real effect would be monotone." On 16 dates it was not; on 31 it is.

### The mechanism, corrected

v1.1.133 attributed the adverse relation to liquidity tracking realized volatility. On the wider
sample that holds only for the *graded* scheme (top quartile 50% realized vol vs 39% at the
bottom). The **legacy** scheme — the one actually shipped — now looks mildly *positive* in
isolation:

| legacy band | assigned | mean return | ivRank |
|---|---|---|---|
| bottom 25% | 18.9% | +0.82% | 53 |
| top 25% | 16.2% | **+1.52%** | **63** |

The last column is the resolution, and it is a different mechanism from the one first recorded:
**liquidity is a noisy proxy for IV rank**, which the income composite already weights at **30**.
Spending 13 further points on a weak echo of a signal already weighted heavily does not add
information — it dilutes the signals that carry it. That reconciles the two sections: liquidity
correlates with good outcomes (Spearman +0.036 legacy, +0.137 graded) *and* weighting it costs
you, because what it correlates through is already in the composite at more than twice the weight.

The v1.1.133 headline conclusion is untouched and now better supported: **liquidity is an
execution-cost property, not an outcome-prediction property.** It answers *can I trade this?*, not
*should I?*, and its correct role is the hard gate it already occupies.

### Standing caveats, unchanged

- **Still one regime** — 31 entry dates in a 6.5-week window, ~155 trades per arm in section B. The
  monotone sweep across five arms is much harder to get by chance than a single pairwise
  comparison, which is why this is reported as settling the question; it is not a multi-regime
  result.
- Volume floored to 0 in both arms (no historical row carries it), `trendNeutral` null in both, as
  in live.
- Held to expiry at intrinsic — no early management, roll, or assignment-then-covered-call leg.
- Absolute section-B numbers are pessimistic and are **not** income's live P&L: the reconstruction
  ranks on composite alone, while live also applies the −20 earnings penalty, the liquidity hard
  gate and the critic. The **delta between arms** is unaffected.
