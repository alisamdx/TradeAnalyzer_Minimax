TradeAnalyzer only. Two more breakdowns for BPS-4. **No application code changed, no rule changed.**

## The second run: both controls fired, pointing opposite ways

| cut | n | median difference (21 − 30) | 21 DTE cheaper |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| pooled | 296 | **+13.6%** | 80 (27%) |
| same expiry type | 60 | **−4.9%** | **45 (75%)** |
| both clear the 25% gate | 78 | **+3.5%** | 23 (29%) |

Type-matched says the shorter expiry is cheaper three times in four. Gate-conditional says the gap
shrinks by 74% but survives, favouring 30. A result that flips with the cut is not yet a result.

**They disagree for a reason that is visible in the 2×2.** Of 296 pairs, **204 are weekly-vs-monthly**
(21 weekly / 30 monthly), 60 weekly-vs-weekly, 32 monthly-vs-weekly, and the both-monthly cell is
structurally empty. Conditioning on "clears the gate at both DTEs" selects for the liquid leg, which
is the monthly — so the gate-conditional row is **still an expiry-type comparison**, measured after
the gate instead of before it. It controls tradability and loses control of type; the type-matched row
does the opposite.

## Added: the intersection

Same expiry type **and** both buckets clearing the gate — the only cell with both controls on.
Everything above it controls one of the two. It may well come back under ten pairs, in which case it
says "too few to say anything" rather than quoting a number, and BPS-4 stays open on n rather than on
a number that cannot bear weight.

## Added: an as-traded comparison, deliberately unpaired

Every cut above is a paired difference, which answers *"on the same name, which expiry costs more"*.
That is not the decision. **A DTE target changes which names clear the gate**, so the book at 21 and
the book at 30 are different populations — and that difference is precisely what is being chosen
between, not a confound to remove.

The first run's 2×2 already implies the magnitude: **102 of 296 candidates clear the cost gate at
21 DTE against 156 at 30** — moving the target would cost about a third of the tradable pool. That
reads straight against BPS-10, where the eligible set the top-5 cut chooses between is already thin
(`premium` fills its five slots every run; `wheel` produces one to three).

The table reports candidates clearing, median cost share, median credit and monthly share per target,
and states in the output that it is **a fact about the rule, not about tenor**: a target that lands on
monthlies inherits the monthlies' liquidity. Both statements are true at once, and keeping them apart
is the whole of BPS-4 — the operational answer and the mechanistic answer are not the same answer.
