# v1.1.260 — 2026-09-17

## QC-210 — bars that predate the ticker's current listing

A new blocking bars check: a stored bar dated before `raw_ticker_reference.list_date`
belongs, by definition, to a previous occupant of the symbol, so the series splices two
entities and no re-fetch can repair it — the provider keys by ticker too.

It is the same fault QC-205 and QC-209 look for, tested on **identity** rather than on
**behaviour**. Those two ask whether the volume regime or the trading gap *looks* like a
splice; this asks the reference data where the listing began. So it catches a reuse whose
two occupants happen to trade at similar volume, which the volume test structurally
cannot.

On the 2026-09-17 data it fires on **11 of 1,082** tickers, including SPCX — the 53,592x
case CLAUDE.md names as the reuse exemplar, and one QC-205 does **not** fire on in
gen-002:

```
SPCX  listed 2026-06-12, bars from 2021-09-13  (1,228 bars)  Space Exploration Technologies
FIG   listed 2025-07-31, bars from 2022-05-17  (1,059)       Figma
JAN   listed 2026-03-20, bars from 2021-09-13    (852)       Janus Living
NIQ   listed 2025-07-23                          (755)       NIQ Global Intelligence
SAIL  listed 2025-02-13                          (649)       SailPoint
SOLS  listed 2025-10-20                          (582)       Solstice Advanced Materials
CAI   listed 2025-06-18                          (381)       Caris Life Sciences
BAM   listed 2022-12-01                        (1,275)       Brookfield Asset Management
```

**Threshold 0, structural, with a base rate behind it.** 11 of 1,082 is 1.0 %, and every
gap exceeds **365 days** — none in the 1-5 or 6-90 day bands. There is no borderline
population for a grace period to protect, so a zero costs nothing. The error is also
one-directional: a `list_date` that is too **early** causes a miss, and only one that is
too **late** could indict good history. The detail line carries the day gap so a future
artefact is ruled out at a glance.

**No automatic remediation**, deliberately. Truncate-before-listing is an operator action
exactly as for QC-205, and several of the eleven are relistings or redomiciles where the
history may legitimately be continuous — ASND, JHX, COLB and SAIL on this sample.
Truncating ASND would delete 1,275 bars of real history.

It also runs in the **backfill's** gate, which is the one job that fetches a ticker's
whole history and therefore the moment a splice becomes visible at all.

## What this replaces: a liquidity floor, measured and rejected

The idea this started from — that QC-205's false positives could be filtered by a prior
**liquidity level** — does not survive its own measurement, and the record should say so.

Only 2 of QC-205's 12 firings are reuse by the listing evidence (JAN). Their prior volume
is 12k and 20k, sitting inside the range of the ten that are not:

```
floor  10k → stops 11 of 12 firings, INCLUDING both genuine reuse cases
floor 100k → stops  4 of 12, none genuine — but leaves BMNR, QXO and APLD
```

A floor either destroys the true positives or removes under half the false ones. The
hypothesis came from three hand-picked rows, which is the "plausible mechanism read off a
few cases" trap this project has recorded before.

## And a measurement about QC-205's threshold, recorded but not acted on

QC-205's 15x is `measured`, from "reuse at 76x and 53,592x against genuine moves at ≤ 5x
— two orders of magnitude apart." On gen-002's own 109 evaluations that gap does **not**
reproduce:

```
n=109   p50 1.92   p90 15.34   p99 403.93   max 467.6   threshold 15
```

A continuum, with the threshold on exactly **p90** — it fires on the top decile by
construction rather than on a gap. Ten of its twelve firings are transformative events on
continuing listings: GME's May 2024 squeeze at 15.3x on 4.9M shares a day, SMMT at 52x,
BMNR at 571x.

The threshold is **left in force**. One generation's 109 evaluations is not grounds for
moving a `measured` threshold on a blocking check whose remedy truncates price and IV
history, and the pilot's distribution review (03 §10) is where that decision belongs. The
measurement is now written next to it so the review starts from data rather than from the
original episode set.

## `qc204-triage.mjs` sorts pre-listing findings out of the queue

The triage now reads `list_date` and buckets a step dated before it as
`pre-listing (QC-210)` **first** — it is not a per-step ruling at all, since one
truncation resolves every finding in that range:

```
move?                    76  (was 95)      pre-listing (QC-210)     26   ← new
illiquid-shell           22                dormant-resume (QC-209)  13
reuse (QC-205)            9  (was 11)      unclear                   2
split?                    2  (was 3)       reuse?                    1
```

SOLS (20) and JAN (6) move out of the individual queue entirely. The one `split?` that
disappeared was a step inside SOLS's sub-penny shell history — spurious, and now
correctly classed.
